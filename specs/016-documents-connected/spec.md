# Documents 5: connected mode

Status: built in ALY-142 (package 5 of the Documents feature, epic ALY-137). Decision: D-042 ("As built (ALY-142)"). It sits on packages 1 and 2 (`specs/011-documents-engine`, `specs/013-documents-sales`) and changes nothing in them that a shop can see: for ALYR-numbered paper the engine's gapless numbers, immutability and hash are as they were. Still hidden: the module `documents` is off for every shop; the back office switches it on for one. The developer's page is `docs/connect-api.md`; the one screen is in the workspace under Connections.

A shop keeps its sales in another POS, accounting or invoicing tool and still wants ALYR's receipt or invoice for its customers (a digital receipt instead of paper), or wants ALYR to carry only part of the paper trail. This package lets that system push sales to ALYR and decides **who owns the numbers**, so nothing is invoiced twice.

## Who owns the numbers: authority

`document_authority` (migration 0043): per shop and per kind of document (all kinds but `statement`, which ALYR draws from its own ledger), `alyr` (the default, no row) or `external`.

With `external`:

* ALYR **never allocates a number** for that kind. A document is made from the other system's data and carries **its** number, `series_key = 'EXT'`, `seq` null, `number_authority = 'external'`. ALYR's counters are not touched and are not gap-checked. The PDF says "Issued by the seller's own system, which gave it the number above. Delivered by ALYR." (A4, A5 and the roll; the registration note, which is about ALYR's own invoices, is not drawn.) The hash commits to `number_authority` (only for external documents, so every earlier hash is unchanged).
* **ALYR's own automatic documents for that kind are skipped.** The choice (the brief left it open: skip, or a non-fiscal "order confirmation"): **skip, and say so**. Every hook already runs behind a savepoint and records `document_skipped` with a reason on the order's or account's activity record; the engine now refuses with `external_authority` (409, both languages) and the hook records exactly that, the sale goes on. An order confirmation is not issued in its place: the order's own WhatsApp messages are as before, and the other system issues the receipt when it rings the sale up and sends it here. The same refusal reaches a person who tries to make or issue such a document by hand (a draft is refused at once, one made before the switch cannot be issued after it), asks for an invoice on request for an order, or records a payment (the payment is kept; its receipt is the other system's job and the answer says `receipt_error: external_authority`).
* **Three places that do not trust each other:** the draft and the issue refuse in the service (`service._require_authority`), numbering is only reached after that, and the database refuses to issue an ALYR-numbered document of a kind the shop gave away, and an outside number for a kind that is ALYR's (`documents_guard`, migration 0043).
* **A number is never used twice in a shop**, whoever numbered it: the unique index on `(shop, number)` is kept (a withdrawn document, below, gives its number back). Giving numbering **back** to ALYR is refused (`authority_number_clash`) when an outside number looks like one ALYR's own counter could reach (it begins with a series prefix of that kind, `RC-...`), because the counter would then be stuck on a refused number; change the series prefix first.
* An outside document does **not** need the shop's CR number or its tax switch: the other system is the issuer. (For the lawyer, ALY-51: whether ALYR rendering an "Invoice" for a shop with no CR number, from another system's data, is acceptable.)

Changing authority is owners and admins, is on the activity record with from and to, and takes effect for the next document; documents already made keep their numbers.

## Connected sales: `POST /api/connect/sales`

`modules/connect/ingest.py`, `routes/connect.py`. In one transaction: lock the `Idempotency-Key` and the sale (`kind` and the other system's `external_sale_id`); replay or refuse; check the terms, currency, authority and number, the day, the totals and the phone; find or make the customer; issue; write the sale (`connect_sales`: key, request hash, the request as sent, the Catalog links); queue delivery; answer.

* **Idempotency.** Same key and body: the first answer (`replayed: true`, 200). Same key, other body: 409 `idempotency_key_reused`. Same sale under another key: same body returns the first answer, other body is 409 `sale_exists`. The engine's own key for the document is fixed by the sale (`connect:` and a hash of kind and sale ID, plus a generation when a withdrawn document held the sale before), so a sale can only ever make one document, and the unique indexes on `connect_sales` are the last guard. Six identical requests, or six requests for one sale under six keys, at the same moment make one document; two sales for one new customer at the same moment take turns on the customer (an advisory lock on the phone; the first run of the tests found the collision on `contacts_tenant_id_phone_key`).
* **Totals.** ALYR works the total out from the lines by the engine's one rounding rule. A difference of up to a minor unit a line is accepted and printed as the document's Rounding line, so the paper's total is the total the till printed; more is `totals_mismatch` with both numbers. A declared tax or discount that disagrees is refused the same way. The other system's own cash rounding (`totals.rounding_minor`) is kept.
* **Payment.** A receipt is paid in full; an invoice may be unpaid or part paid; a kind that records no payment refuses one. Methods are the engine's.
* **Credit notes** name the earlier sale (`credits_sale_id`), which ALYR must have been sent; the engine's rules hold (cannot pass the original, voids it when covered; for an outside credit note: `credit_note_external`).
* **The customer** is found by phone (international format required) or made, with the sale's name and the language hint; an existing customer is never renamed. The shop's email, when given and deliverable, is kept on a customer that has none.
* **Catalog link.** A line whose SKU matches a live item or size is recorded in `connect_sales.matches` and in the answer. **No stock moves** (the other system owns stock): a test counts the stock movements.

## Consent and delivery

`modules/connect/terms.py`, `modules/documents/delivery.py`.

* **The shop's terms.** Before a key can be made, an owner or admin confirms four sentences in the settings (versioned, `document_settings.connect_terms_*`): the shop has each customer's permission to be sent their receipts, invoices and credit notes on WhatsApp and by email; it will send only those; a customer who says stop is not messaged again; a document its own system numbers is issued by it. A key made before a new version keeps working (a change of wording never stops a till); a new key waits for the new acceptance.
* **The consent.** When a sale asks for delivery (`send: true`, or leaving it out when the shop's settings send automatically) and the customer's service consent is `unknown`, ALYR records `consents (purpose service, status granted, source connected_sale, wording)`: the shop's assertion, the terms' version and the key's public prefix, never its secret. Marketing is never granted this way. A customer already `granted` is left alone (one row, not one per sale); **`revoked` stays revoked**: the shop's assertion never overrides a stop. No delivery is asked for, no consent is recorded.
* **Delivery** reuses ALY-139's machinery and states: one automatic delivery per document and channel, WhatsApp through `Outbound.send` (consent, the 24-hour window and the limit are checked there), email with the PDF, the hosted link. `send: true` sends on WhatsApp whatever the shop's automatic settings say; `false` sends nothing; an import sends only when the shop ticks "send".
* **Templates not approved.** The three document templates are written and **not submitted** to Meta (nothing in this package submits them). A new setting `ALYR_DOCUMENT_TEMPLATES_APPROVED` (default off, like `ALYR_REPORT_CARD_TEMPLATE`) makes the delivery job say, outside the 24-hour window, at once: delivery `blocked`, reason `template_not_approved`, nothing sent to Meta, `document.failed` to the shop's webhooks with the hosted link, and an email delivery to the customer when they have an address. A customer who said stop is told `opted_out`, not this. Inside the window the PDF goes as before. The founder switches the setting on after Meta approves all three.
* **Webhook events from delivery:** blocked, a WhatsApp report of failed, and a message that exhausted its retries produce `document.failed`; WhatsApp's `delivered` (the first of delivered or read) produces `document.delivered`; an email failure produces `document.failed`.

## API keys

`modules/connect/keys.py`, `api_keys` (migration 0043). `alyr_k1_<8 hex prefix>_<43 characters>`; the SHA-256 of the secret is stored (a 256-bit random value needs no slow hash, as sessions and sign-in codes), compared with `hmac.compare_digest`, and the same work is done whether or not the prefix exists. Scopes `sales:write`, `documents:read`, `webhooks`; up to 10 active keys; revoke is immediate and idempotent; `last_used_at` is noted (at most once a minute); a revoked key's state is only told to someone who holds its secret. A key authenticates a machine: the workspace's routes read a cookie and ignore the header, the machine's routes read the header and ignore the cookie, and the shop is set from the key, so row-level security does the rest. The policy on `api_keys` lets one row be read by its prefix before the shop is known (the way `memberships` lets sign-in find a person's shops) and writes stay inside the shop.

**Rate limit:** one row, one atomic update, a one-minute window per key (120 to start, 1 to 1,000); 429 with `Retry-After` and `X-RateLimit-*` headers; a refused call still counts. No Redis, no new table. Fixed windows allow up to twice the limit across a boundary.

**Audit:** key made and revoked, authority changed, terms accepted, webhook added, changed, replaced, removed, switched off, tested, a sale received (via, key prefix, kind) and an import applied or undone, all on the activity record (`subject = document`) with who; what a key does to webhooks is under the key's name.

## Outgoing webhooks

`modules/connect/webhooks.py`, `ssrf.py`, `webhook_endpoints` and `webhook_deliveries`, job `webhook_send`.

* **Events:** `document.issued`, `document.delivered`, `document.failed`, `document.cancelled` (a document voided by credit notes, or an import's document withdrawn), `payment.recorded`. A delivery row and its job are written **in the transaction of the event** (an event that rolled back is never sent, one that committed is never lost). The data carries no customer name, phone or address.
* **Signature:** headers `X-Alyr-Event`, `X-Alyr-Delivery` (the event ID, the same on every retry), `X-Alyr-Timestamp`, `X-Alyr-Signature: sha256=<HMAC-SHA256(secret, "<timestamp>.<body>")>`. The secret is shown once and can be replaced. (It is stored so ALYR can sign; it cannot be hashed.)
* **Retries:** eight attempts, waits 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, 30 min, 1 h, 3 h, 6 h (the handler enqueues its own next job, so the job queue's one backoff is not in the way); the log keeps attempts, the last status and the first 500 characters of the answer.
* **Auto-disable:** ten failures in a row and no success for 24 hours switches the address off, on the activity record, with an email to the owners and admins (host only, never the full address) in both languages when email is set up. One bad evening switches nothing off.
* **SSRF guard** (`ssrf.py`): https only (http and local only on a developer machine with both `ALYR_ENV=local` and `ALYR_WEBHOOK_ALLOW_LOCAL`); a domain name, never an address written as numbers in any spelling; no credentials; port 443 or 1024 and above; local names refused; **every address the name resolves to must be public** (loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, multicast, reserved, IPv4-mapped, NAT64, 6to4 and Teredo are not); the look-up is repeated **at send time**; the connection goes to the IP that was checked, with the name in `Host` and in the TLS handshake (no rebinding); **redirects are never followed**; 3 s to connect, 10 s in all; at most 2 KB of the answer is read; no proxy from the environment.

## CSV import of sales

`modules/connect/sale_import_plan.py` (pure), `sale_imports.py`, `sale_imports` and `sale_import_templates`. The Catalog import's file reading (`items/sheets.py`: UTF-8, UTF-16 or Windows-1256; comma, semicolon or tab; Excel; nothing calculated), its money and date reading (`items/values.py`) and its habits are reused. One row per line, rows with one sale ID are one sale; columns found by name in English or Arabic, a mapping by the file's own headers, **remembered templates** that are applied to the next file whose headers fit; a downloadable template (English or Arabic); **a preview of every row** with its number and the words in both languages (including the checks that need the database: a number taken, a sale already received, the engine's rules for the paper, via `ingest.accept(dry=True)`); apply carries the preview's hash and makes **everything in one transaction through the same door as the API**, so it is idempotent (an existing sale is skipped); a file holds up to 200 sales and 2,000 rows (a PDF is drawn for each inside the transaction).

**Undo** of the newest import: its documents are **withdrawn** (migration 0043: `withdrawn_at`, `withdrawn_by`, `withdrawn_reason`; the one change the immutability guard allows besides voiding, only for a document another system numbered, only while no delivery other than a blocked one exists and no credit note points at it, and only those columns change) and kept, marked, never deleted, out of the lists and the hosted page, free to be imported again with the same numbers. Refused (with words) for documents ALYR numbered (a gapless counter and issued paper are never taken back), for an import that sent anything, and for an import that voided other documents. A withdrawal is a status-like flag in the row, so every read of the document shows it.

### Mutation checks

Each thing below was broken on purpose, the tests that should notice were run on a scratch database in Docker, and the break was undone (20 Oct 2026). Every break was caught.

| What was broken | What failed |
|---|---|
| **Idempotency:** the `Idempotency-Key` is not looked up (`ingest._replay`) | 2 of 31 sale tests: the same key with another body (it was `sale_exists` instead of `idempotency_key_reused`), and two different sales under one key at the same moment. A retry of the same body still passed, because the sale's own lookup catches it: the two lookups cover each other |
| **Idempotency:** the sale (kind and external ID) is not locked | 1: six requests for one sale under six keys at the same moment (a second document, or a unique-index failure) |
| **Idempotency:** the key is not locked | **First run: nothing failed**: the sale's lock already makes six identical requests take turns. That showed a gap, and a test was added (two different sales under one key at the same moment: one document and a 409, not a failure on the unique index); with the lock out it now fails |
| **Idempotency:** the request's hash is ignored when a key comes back | 2: the same key with another body, and two sales under one key at once |
| **Authority:** the service no longer asks who numbers a kind (`service._require_authority`) | 5 of 20 authority tests: no door allocates a number (draft, issue, on request), the hook for a paid order, the hook for a credit sale, a payment by hand. The database's own refusal is still there, which is why the engine's failures are a refused request and not paper |
| **Authority:** the database no longer refuses an ALYR number for a handed-over kind (the guard in migration 0043) | 1: `test_the_database_refuses_an_alyr_number_for_a_kind_the_shop_gave_away` (which takes the application's check away first, so it proves the database alone) |

## Not built, on purpose

Named POS connectors (by demand); the accountant export (ALY-143); a payment QR; a screen for the lists of sales (ALY-146); a webhook for `document.draft`; the hosted page does not say a document is the other system's (the API sends `external` for it); stock moves for an outside sale; tax-inclusive prices; a per-key IP allow-list; a Redis rate limiter; webhook payload versions; a second signing secret during rotation.

## Open questions

1. **Founder, Meta.** Submit `document_receipt`, `document_invoice` and `document_credit_note` (English and Arabic) and then set `ALYR_DOCUMENT_TEMPLATES_APPROVED`. Until then a customer who has not written to the shop in 24 hours gets email or nothing, and the shop's other system is told why.
2. **Lawyer (ALY-51).** Whether a shop's confirmation in a settings screen is enough as the opt-in for a first transactional WhatsApp message to a customer who never wrote to the shop (WhatsApp's own rule is opt-in; Qatar's privacy law asks for a record, which `consents` is). Whether ALYR may render and deliver an "Invoice" from another system's data for a shop with no CR number. The retention of a withdrawn document (kept, as all).
3. **Engineering.** A document is drawn inside the transaction, so an import of 200 sales holds the transaction for a while (and, for an ALYR-numbered kind, its numbering row lock): draw before the lock when a shop imports often (spec 011's note). The webhook secret is stored in the clear so ALYR can sign: an encryption key in the secret store would be the next step.
