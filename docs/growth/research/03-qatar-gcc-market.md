### 1. Meta click-to-WhatsApp ads

**Free messaging window (verified from Meta's own pricing page)**
- When someone messages you from a click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Facebook Page button on the Android or iOS app (not desktop or web), the normal 24-hour reply window opens. If the business replies within those 24 hours, the reply is free and a 72-hour free window opens. Any message type, including templates, is free during it. Source: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing (no page date; opened 18 Oct 2026; its latest dated note is 12 May 2026).
- Meta moved from per-conversation to per-message pricing on 1 Jul 2025. Older "cost per conversation" figures no longer match how billing works. Same source.
- Qatar (+974) now has its own rate line instead of the "Rest of Middle East" rate. Its utility and authentication rates went up on 1 Jul 2026, and it paid regional rates until 30 Jun 2026. Other changes:
  - Saudi Arabia: higher marketing rate from 1 Apr 2026.
  - UAE: higher marketing rate from 1 Oct 2025.
  - Iraq, Kuwait and Oman: expected to get their own rates from 1 Oct 2026.
  - AED and SAR became billing currencies on 1 Apr 2026.
  - Same source.
- Salesforce's rate card says the same thing in another form: the ad itself is billed normally and only the conversation it starts is free. https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/assets/pdf/WhatsApp_Business-Messaging-Rate-Card-Aug-1-2025.pdf (Aug 2025).

**Ad cost benchmarks for Qatar and the Gulf: weak data, treat all as unverified**
- **No Qatar-specific CPM, CPC or cost per conversation found.** Superads has a Qatar CPM page (Oct 2025 to Sep 2026, from about $3B of spend in its customers' accounts), but the numbers sit in a live widget I couldn't read. The only text figure is a general "$5 to $18 in most industries". https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/qatar (opened 18 Oct 2026).
- **UAE Meta CPM** averaged about $19.20 from May 2025 to May 2026, against a $19.81 global median. UAE e-commerce averaged $15.51, with a low of $9.44 in Mar 2025 and a high of $23.77 in Jan 2026. Superads (as reported in search results): https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/united-arab-emirates (2026).
- **A blog puts GCC CPMs at "$3 to $8"** with no method or date. This conflicts with the Superads figures, so it is unverified. https://upgrowth.in/?p=63703.
- **A UAE agency guide** says CPC is AED 4 to 32, CPM AED 1 to 120, and a typical SMB daily budget is AED 50 to 150. It is a vendor source, so unverified. https://blog.digitalnexa.com/instagram-advertising-in-the-uae-what-businesses-in-dubai-abu-dhabi-need-to-know (updated Feb 2025).
- **Click-to-WhatsApp CPC in Dubai of AED 1.5 to 3.5** comes from one vendor blog, so unverified. https://www.messagecentral.com/blog/whatsapp-marketing-uae (undated).
- **One Doha agency** lists a package of QAR 4,500 a month including Meta ads and management. It is the agency's own price, not a market rate. https://leadsmarketing.run.place/ (undated).

**Gulf case study (Meta's own)**
- Arabian Automobiles Company, the Nissan distributor for Dubai and the Northern Emirates and part of AW Rostamani, A/B-tested click-to-WhatsApp ads (with a WhatsApp Flows test-drive booking) against ads sending people to its website. It reported 2x leads, 56% lower cost per lead and 4% lower cost per link click. The campaign ran 17 to 30 Apr 2025. This is a large company, not a small one, and the results are self-reported. https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/success-stories/arabian-automobiles-company/.
- **No small-business case study from Qatar, Saudi Arabia or the UAE was found.** Meta's product page lists four success stories, none from the Gulf, and makes no claim about the 72-hour window. https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/ads-that-click-to-whatsapp/ (© 2026).
- 360dialog claims 70% of marketers get up to 3x return on ad spend from click-to-WhatsApp ads. It is a vendor survey, so unverified. https://360dialog.com/resources/whatsapp-marketing-report.

**Fit for ALYR:** The 72-hour free window is a strong selling point, because ALYR's inbox can promise "reply within 24 hours and the next 3 days of messages are free". Ad costs need a small test campaign in Qatar. Published benchmarks are not reliable enough to plan with.

### 1. Qatar: advertising and influencer rules

**Influencer licence: none in force yet. Only a proposal.**
- On 23 Dec 2024 the Shura Council (Qatar's advisory council) agreed to send the Cabinet a proposal for a "structured regulatory framework to govern digital media content". Under it, influencers would be licensed by the relevant state authorities. The article reports no Cabinet decision or law. https://marhaba.qa/licensing-framework-for-social-media-influencers-proposed-by-shura-council/ (24 Dec 2024)
- I found nothing showing it was enacted in 2025 or 2026: **unverified**.

**Ministry of Culture advertising and PR licence: the closest existing rule.**
- **What it covers:** designing ads, promoting goods or services, campaigns and PR work. The article ties it to "Decree Law No. 16 of 1993".
- **Fees:** QAR 5,000 to issue and QAR 5,000 to renew each year. These were cut from QAR 25,000 and QAR 10,000 in January 2024. The licence lasts one year.
- **Who can apply:** individuals must be Qatari; companies apply through an authorised signatory.
- **Paid posts:** no creator-specific rule says every paid post needs the licence.
- Source: https://www.qatarliving.com/en/article/content-creation-social-media-qatar (4 Oct 2026). It is a secondary source, and I couldn't check the fees on the Ministry's own site.

**Media City Qatar:** its 2024 agreement with the QFC (Qatar Financial Centre) licenses media companies, not individual influencers. https://www.qfc.qa/en/media-centre/news/list/qfc-media-city-mou (2024; exact date not confirmed)

**Promotions and discounts:** the Ministry of Commerce consumer page says you can "Apply for a license to run promotional discounts and seasonal offers". The application is at https://ucs.moci.gov.qa/login. http://www.moci.gov.qa/en/consumer (seen 18 Oct 2026)

**Consumer Protection Law No. 8 of 2008:** the same Ministry page names it as the governing law and links the law and its Executive Regulations: https://www.moci.gov.qa/sites/default/files/2026-06/moci1.pdf (uploaded June 2026). I couldn't extract the PDF's text. These article contents are **unverified**:
- price display
- a ban on misleading advertising
- Arabic invoices and labels
- penalties

**Arabic language:**
- **Law No. 7 of 2019:**
  - Company and trade names must be in Arabic. Internationally known foreign names may stay if they also appear in Arabic.
  - Trademarks must be in Arabic. Another language may be added, but Arabic must be the most prominent.
  - Fines go up to QAR 50,000, and parties had six months to comply.
  - Source: https://qatar-tribune.com/news-details/id/152201 (15 Jan 2019)
- **Ads under Law 7/2019:** a secondary summary says the law requires Arabic for "advertising (audio and visual)" by public bodies and by private organisations and associations. **Unverified**: the source page now returns 404 and I couldn't read the official text.
- **Ministry of Commerce 2016 guidance:** labels, menus, invoices and advertisements should be in Arabic, optionally with an English translation next to it. https://www.globalizationpartners.com/2019/08/13/arabic-translation-requirements-in-qatar/ (13 Aug 2019)
- **Law No. 1 of 2012 on Advertisements:**
  - The definition of an advertisement is broad: any means of informing the public about goods or activities.
  - You need a licence from the municipality before displaying an ad.
  - Whether it covers online ads is not stated.
  - Source: https://vlex.com/vid/qatar-s-new-advertising-law-386666884 (2012)
  - The rule that Arabic must be the main language on billboards, with fines up to QAR 20,000, is **unverified** because the Doha News page blocked access.
- **Price display in QAR:** **unverified**. I found no source.

### 2. UAE, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait: permits

**UAE Advertiser Permit (UAE Media Council)**
- **Who needs it:** anyone posting promotional content, paid or unpaid.
- **Cost and term:** free for the first three years. Valid one year and renewable.
- **Display rules:** the permit number must be shown on the account, and ads may only go out through accounts registered to the permit.
- **Exemption that matters for ALYR:** promoting your own product, or a company you own, through your own account.
- **Visitors:** they need a three-month permit through a licensed UAE agency.
- **Applications:** through uaemc.gov.ae. That site's security certificate had expired, so I couldn't check it directly.
- Sources: https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/07/30/uae-social-media-advertiser-permit/ and https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/uae-launches-advertiser-permit-for-online-advertisements?amp=1 (both 30 Jul 2025)
- **In force from 1 Feb 2026**, under Federal Media Law No. 55 of 2023 and Cabinet Resolution No. 42 of 2025. Holders must follow 20 content standards, and brands must check that the creators they hire hold permits. Fines (AED):

| Violation | Fine (AED) |
|---|---|
| Content violations | 5,000 – 1,000,000 |
| Violations affecting state interests or national security | 50,000 – 500,000 |
| Operating without a licence | 5,000 – 10,000 |
| False or misleading information | 10,000 first offence, 40,000 repeat |

  Source: https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/?p=5796 (2 Feb 2026)
- **AED 2 million for a repeat offence:** reported in an early search summary but **unverified**. The article above doesn't mention it.

**Saudi Arabia: Mawthooq licence**
- Now issued by GAMR (General Authority for Media Regulation), formerly GCAM.
- **Cost and term:** SAR 15,000 (about USD 4,000) for three years.
- **Who needs it:** any creator posting paid promotion in the Kingdom, including foreign creators targeting Saudi audiences.
- **Penalties:** up to SAR 5 million and possible prison; brands that use unlicensed creators can also be penalised.
- **2025–2026 changes:** the article describes none.
- Sources:
  - https://everything-pr.com/mawthooq-the-saudi-arabia-influencer-licensing-regulator (19 Jun 2026)
  - https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2022/09/new-social-media-requirements-for-saudi-arabia (Sep 2022)
- **Foreign creators:** they must first get an investment licence, then a trade licence and media licence, then Mawthooq. https://communicateonline.me/news/influencer-marketing-by-brand-ripplr-the-latest-updates-on-influencer-legislations-and-their-impact-on-businesses/ (date not checked)

**Kuwait:** **unverified**. I found nothing before the search limit.

### 4. Data protection and marketing consent

**Qatar: Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL), Law No. 13 of 2016**
- Unsolicited direct marketing is banned. Electronic marketing needs prior consent that is "affirmative, explicit and unambiguous"; pre-ticked boxes may not count.
- Each message must name the sender, say it is direct marketing, and give a working address for opting out. Consent can be withdrawn at any time.
- The regulator is NCGAA (National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs) within the National Cyber Security Agency, which also issues guidelines. Fines go up to 5 million (presumably QAR, about USD 1.4 million).
- Collecting children's data needs a parent's express approval.
- Source: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=electronic-marketing&c=QA (updated 17 Jan 2024)
- Still **unverified**:
  - the article numbers (Article 22 for consent, Article 23 for sender identity)
  - fines broken down by article
  - a body called the "National Data Privacy Office"

**UAE**
- The PDPL (Decree-Law 45/2021) gives people the right to object to direct marketing; the source cites Art. 15 and Art. 17 inconsistently.
- Cabinet Decision 56/2024, Art. 4, requires consent before marketing messages on social media.
- The consumer-protection law for e-commerce, Decree-Law 14/2023, Arts. 5–6, lets consumers choose their marketing channels.
- When the page was last updated, the PDPL's own penalties had not been issued.
- Source: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=electronic-marketing&c=AE (update date not checked)

**Saudi Arabia**
- The PDPL and its Implementing Regulations set specific direct-marketing conditions. Fines go up to SAR 5 million, doubled for repeat offences; misusing sensitive data can bring up to two years in prison and SAR 3 million. https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=electronic-marketing&c=SA (updated 11 Feb 2026)
- Article 25 requiring consent plus a way to stop promotional messages is **unverified** (my own knowledge).

### 5. Cultural and religious content; Cybercrime Law
- **UAE:** content must meet the Media Council's 20 content standards (Middle East Briefing, 2 Feb 2026).
- **Qatar's direction of travel:** the Shura proposal aims at content "aligned with Qatar's values and national identity" and at curbing hate speech and misleading claims (marhaba.qa, 24 Dec 2024).

### Patterns that seem to work

1. **Founder and staff posts beat brand posts.** SkipCash's CEO got 146 reactions against 17 for the company post on the same news. Zoko's staff reposts get 61 to 89, against 3 to 4 for the brand.
2. **Partnership and integration news gets the most engagement on business LinkedIn.** Tamara and Tamimi got 345. SkipCash's agreements with Foodics and Salla did well. Tamara and SkipCash both lean on Salla and Zid integrations.
3. **National and religious moments in Arabic do very well.** Salla's prayer post got 158, about 10 times its usual. Tamara's Saudi National Day posts got 210 and 170. Zid ran a National Day offer. Ramadan was not checked.
4. **Gulf event presence makes the strongest content for WhatsApp tools.** Gallabox's Abu Dhabi posts get 44 to 54, against 2 to 5 for its product posts. Other examples: Money20/20 Middle East (Tamara 216), QSTP summits and Customer Experience Live Dubai.
5. **Arabic short video works as a steady drumbeat, but views are modest.** Zid posts Shorts almost daily at 100 to 1,300 views, using a podcast, a "radio" series and merchant founder clips. This builds familiarity rather than going viral.
6. **Merchant success stories run everywhere, for steady but middling engagement.** Examples: Foodics, Salla, Gallabox's carousel and Booksy's video series.
7. **Comparison and SEO articles are for search, not social.** respond.io gets 5 to 10 reactions on these.
8. **Partner and affiliate programmes are standard.** Salla pays up to SAR 100K in commission. Foodics runs referral, reseller and customer-to-customer referral programmes. Wati has an Arabic affiliate page. Gallabox and Interakt each run partner and affiliate programmes. Kommo reaches Saudi Arabia through its partner network.
9. **Local proof logos matter more than pricing in local currency.** Mall of Qatar (Gallabox), Care n Cure (Zoko) and Qatar Red Crescent (Wati, Dibsy) all appear. Most tools price in USD; Fatora is the only one pricing in QAR.
10. **The Qatar bar is low.** Local SMB fintechs have about 3K to 9K LinkedIn followers and 0.7K to 12K on Instagram. A few thousand engaged followers would make a new Qatar tool visible.
11. **In-person roadshows are a proven SMB channel.** Interakt's city roadshows with SMBConnect match ALYR's plan to sell in person.
12. **Platform mix in Saudi Arabia includes Snapchat and TikTok.** Zid and Tamara list them in their footers. Tamara and Foodics split accounts by language or country.
