# The sales demo: bakeries, sweets and home kitchens

The 15-minute demo the founder runs in a shop, on his laptop. It is not a slide deck and not a site tour: the owner sees their own bakery's Thursday night, and the same night with ALYR. Agreed with the founder on 29 Oct 2026. Linear ALY-263. Built at `/demo/bakery`.

## Decided (29 Oct)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Device | The founder's laptop. He drives; the owner taps at two moments (their pains, the custom cake) |
| Language | English first, Arabic one tap away, everywhere |
| Price | Not shown. The demo ends on booking a check-up; the price comes in a quote from the back office (D-032) |
| Bundle shown as "Set up by ALYR" | Google Business Profile setup, and review requests after each order |
| Tone | Confident, specific, a real night in a real shop. Never generic |

## Tone rules

- Their bakery's name, their night, their customer's name on the cake. Never "businesses like yours".
- Short lines, one idea per screen, times on a clock (11:52 pm), money in QAR.
- State it: "It's on tomorrow's list." Never "it can help with".
- No "AI", "smart", "seamless", "solution", "platform", "unlock".
- **Honest by label.** Every scene says Built, Set up by ALYR or Sample:
  - the leak figure comes from the owner's own three numbers and says "your estimate";
  - the weekly numbers are a sample week;
  - no invented market statistics or customers.
- **WhatsApp line until Meta approves ALYR as a Tech Provider:** "Works on WhatsApp, the app your customers already use." Not "keep your number and app" (that comes with Tech Provider, ALY-39).

## The script

Each scene: what's on screen, then what the founder says (shown to him only in presenter notes, never to the owner).

### 1. Their bakery (0:00)
- **Screen:** black, white type. "[Their bakery]. Thursday before Eid. 11:47 pm." Under it: "14 unread chats." The founder types the name and picks the shop's colour on a small setup panel first.
- **Says:** "I'm not going to show you an app. I'm going to show you your Thursday night, and then the same night with us."

### 2. Where the money goes (1:00)
- **Screen:** "Three numbers. Yours, not ours."
  - Messages a day, how many arrive after closing, the average order.
  - Then one line: "About QAR [x] a month you never saw", labelled "your estimate". The formula is shown on tap: after-hours messages × days × share that would have ordered × average order, with the share set at a stated, conservative value the founder can change.
  - Then ten pain cards, in the owner's words:
    - "Someone ordered at midnight. I saw it at 9."
    - "Twenty messages for one birthday cake."
    - "I said yes to too many orders for Eid."
    - "I'm still checking transfer screenshots."
    - "'Is it ready?' all day."
    - "'Is the pistachio box still there?'"
    - "The delivery app took 25%."
    - "The café pays me 'next week' every week."
    - "I don't know what sold best."
    - "Nobody finds me on Google."
- **Says:** "Tap the ones that happened to you this month. Good. We'll spend the next ten minutes on exactly those."
- The tapped pains decide the order of scene 4, and are kept for the end (saved to the sales tool when that exists).

### 3. The order that would have been lost (3:00)
- **Screen:** split screen. Left, a customer's phone; right, the owner's workspace.
  - **11:52 pm:** "Salam, do you have a cake for tomorrow? Birthday, chocolate, name on it."
  - The reply arrives in two seconds with the menu and an order page.
  - The owner builds the cake: size, flavour, the name "Noor 7", pickup 4:00 pm. A pay link, then Paid.
  - **Right side:** the order lands, tomorrow's kitchen list writes itself, and Thursday's chocolate cakes read 11 of 12.
  - **Then:** a second customer takes the twelfth; a third gets "Full for Thursday. Next free: Saturday."
- **Says:**
  - "You were asleep. The order was taken, paid, and it's on tomorrow's list. You didn't type a word."
  - "That's the Eid problem, solved before it starts. You set the number once. ALYR says no for you, politely, and offers the next day."

### 4. Your pains, solved (8:00)
Only the cards they tapped, in their order; each flips from before to after with one live moment.
- **"Is it ready?":** 3:40 pm, "Your cake is ready for pickup", and the tracking page. *"They stop asking because they already know."*
- **"Is the pistachio box still there?":** it sells out, the page says Sold out, someone taps "Tell me when it's back", and the next morning at 9 one message goes. *"You restock, they come back."*
- **"The café pays next week":** the café's account reads QAR 1,840, 38 days, and a reminder goes, firmer because the debt is older. *"You don't chase. The account does."*
  - The café also orders at its own trade prices (price groups). *"Your cafés see their price, your customers see theirs."*
- **"The office wants an invoice":** one tap, a numbered invoice in Arabic and English. *"That's a corporate client you can now say yes to."*
- **"The delivery app took 25%":** their own link next to the app, the same QAR 180 order, "QAR 45 kept", labelled "your estimate". *"Your regulars don't need an app between you."*
- **"Twenty messages for one cake":** points back to scene 3's cake builder.
- **"Transfer screenshots":** the pay link and the Paid mark. Card payments go live with the shop's own payment provider; label it so.
- **"I don't know what sold best":** goes to scene 7.
- **"Nobody finds me":** goes to scene 6.

### 5. One order, the whole business (10:00): the products working together
The synergy scene. One order is drawn travelling through the whole system, lighting each product in turn, in the suite's order:
- **ALYR Chat:** the chat, the order page, tracking, and one inbox for the team.
- **ALYR Shelf:** the item's stock drops by one; the daily limit counts it.
- **ALYR Docs:** the receipt is numbered and kept.
- **ALYR Accounts:** if it is the café, it goes on their account at their price.
- **ALYR Bookings:** cake tastings and catering consultations, booked with a reminder two hours before.
- **ALYR Agent:** it is counted in today's numbers and in Sunday's report.

**Then the infrastructure view: before and after.**
- **Before:** the notebook, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, the bank app's screenshots, an Excel sheet, the delivery app, the calculator, each a separate island.
- **After:** one workspace, one customer record, one set of numbers, each person on the team with their own sign-in and their own work.

**Then "it grows with you":**
- a second branch under the same account;
- staff roles;
- more products switched on when needed;
- the monthly review.

- **Says:** "This isn't six apps. It's one system: every order updates your stock, your receipts, your accounts and your numbers at once. You run the bakery, it keeps the books straight."

### 6. Be found (11:00): Set up by ALYR
- **Screen:**
  - A phone searching "cake shop near me": their bakery on Google Maps with an Order button that opens their ALYR page.
  - After Thursday's pickup, one message: "Thank you, Noor's mum. A 10-second review helps us a lot." A five-star review appears.
  - The counter QR card and the Instagram bio link.
- **Says:** "Every happy customer becomes the reason the next one finds you. We set up your Google profile, and the review request goes after every pickup."

### 7. Your Sunday (12:30)
- **Screen:** Sunday, 9:00 am, the report card on WhatsApp for their week (sample week, labelled), then the Agent home: what needs them today, and why.
- **Says:** "One message on Sunday tells you how the week went. No spreadsheet, no guessing."

### 8. How we work (13:30)
- **Screen:** four steps, nothing else.
  1. We come to your shop.
  2. We set it up with you.
  3. You go live.
  4. Every month we sit with your numbers.
- **Says:** "You don't learn software. We bring it, set it up, and stay."
- **Objections, one tap each:**
  - "I already have WhatsApp Business." → "Works on WhatsApp, the app your customers already use."
  - "Is my data safe?" → "Your customers are yours. Our staff never read your chats."
  - "What if I want to stop?" → "Month to month, 7 days' notice."
  - "I'm not technical." → "Good. You don't need to be."

### 9. Next step (14:30)
- **Screen:** "Let's do your Thursday properly." A QR code to a test line, and "Book your check-up".
- **Says:** "Scan this. That's your bakery answering, right now. Let's book the day we set it up."

## How it is built

- **Where:** `/demo/bakery` under `apps/web/app/(product)`, `noindex`, not linked from the site.
  - Self-contained and client-side, with fictional data from the Qirfa Bakery sample, renamed to the owner's bakery.
  - No API calls are needed to run it; it works offline once loaded (pre-cache it).
- **Reuse the real pieces where they fit:**
  - the Agent dashboard in showcase mode (`apps/web/app/_agent`, samples `bakery.*.json`);
  - the report card samples;
  - the chat drawings and the order-page look (`(product)/_customer`, `start/Chat.tsx`);
  - design tokens (`design/tokens.json`).
- **Controls:**
  - arrow keys, space and a presenter clicker (Page Up and Down) move between scenes and beats;
  - a slim progress line, and a "jump to scene" menu;
  - the language switch;
  - "N" toggles presenter notes (the "Says" lines) for the founder;
  - "R" restarts.
- **Look:** Carbon and Volt, the working-drawing style, the 45° signature motion. Big type, one idea per screen, laptop first at 1440 × 900 and 1280 × 800. Light theme by default (shop lighting), dark available.
- **Arabic:** full parity, right to left, a draft for native review (ALY-66).
- **Later sectors** (sweets, home kitchens, then salons) are data and words: the scene engine stays.
