**What they have in common.**
- **Self-serve first.** Nearly every main button says "start free", "install" or "book a demo". None says a person will come to the shop.
- **Proof by scale:** logo walls, customer counts, ratings, awards. ALYR has none of these and must not fake them.
- **Prices behind a link.** Only Take App shows one, in USD.
- **AI is the headline** for the WhatsApp tools (Wati, respond.io, Interakt, LetsBot).
- **The Qatar pages localise the headline only.** LetsBot and Ordable still sell a self-serve trial.

**Five gaps ALYR can own.**
1. **Set up in your shop, by a person.** No competitor's homepage promises "we do the tech". Make the visit and the setup the way in.
2. **The price on the page, in QAR.** Nobody in this set shows a QAR price on the homepage. "From QAR 449 a month" is a trust signal on its own, and it matches what the founder says at the counter.
3. **Risk reversal instead of logos.** Where others show 16,000 customers, ALYR offers a paid first month with the money back if an agreed result is missed. "No customers yet" becomes "we carry the risk".
4. **The owner's control, not "growth".** Competitors sell engagement, ROI and AI. ALYR sells what the owner actually worries about (D-028): who answered, who dropped the order, and what the week brought, delivered every Sunday.
5. **One partner for the chat and the rest of the tech.** Agencies sell services and software companies sell software. Nobody holds the middle: a subscription that includes looking after the owner's other tech (within the licence).

## 2. Homepage strategy

**Who lands here, most valuable first.**
1. **The owner the founder has just visited.** On their phone, minutes or a day after the demo, often showing a partner or manager. They want to know: is this a real company, is the price what he told me, and how do I reach him?
2. **A referral.** A friend said "they set it up for us". They want to know: what is it, does it fit a business like mine, and what does it cost?
3. **Someone from social.** They saw a reel and know less. They want to know: what does it do, can I trust it, and what is the first step?

Also: programme reviewers (startup credits, SMEs Go Digital), who check the site against the application.

**What they must believe.**
- **In 10 seconds:** ALYR is a local partner that sets up the tech for businesses like mine, starting with my WhatsApp, and I can message them with one tap.
- **In 60 seconds:** I can see what it does on a phone. I know the price. They do the setup and the training. My first month carries no real risk. They are honest about being new.
- **Before messaging:** a message commits me to nothing, a person replies in my language, and nothing here pretends to be WhatsApp or Meta.

**The page's one job:** get the owner to send ALYR the first WhatsApp message. Every section either earns that tap or removes a reason not to. The quieter second job: say exactly what the founder says in person, so the visit and the page agree.

**Build rules.** Phone first: most visits happen on a phone, standing up. One button, repeated, always with the same words. Real product screens, labelled as samples. The price is visible in the hero facts and again in its own section.

## 5. Hero headline alternatives, ranked

1. **"Go digital. Without the IT team."** Subhead as in 3.1: "ALYR sets up and looks after the technology for owner-run businesses in Qatar. We start with your WhatsApp…" *Why first:* the founder's own line, already live and on the leave-behinds, so visit and page agree; it echoes "SMEs Go Digital", and the subhead does the concrete work.
2. **"We do the tech. You run the business."** Subhead: "ALYR sets up your WhatsApp for orders, bookings and payments, trains your team and stays on call. Then we look after the rest of your tech." *Why:* it states the deal and the in-person promise most plainly. It is weaker as a headline because it does not say what changes. Best for the leave-behind cover.
3. **"Your WhatsApp, set up as your shop counter."** Subhead: "Orders, bookings and payments in the chat, your whole team on one number, and your numbers every Sunday. Set up for you by ALYR." *Why:* the most concrete and visual. But it makes ALYR read as a WhatsApp tool, which D-026 rejected for the homepage. Use it on `/chat`.
4. **"Know every order your team takes."** Subhead: "ALYR puts your WhatsApp orders, bookings and payments on one screen, with a name on each, and sends you the week's numbers every Sunday." *Why:* the owner-control angle no competitor uses (D-028). It is narrower and leads with oversight rather than selling more. Test it in ads.
5. **"Every order, booking and payment. In the chat."** Subhead: "Set up for you by ALYR, in Arabic and English, from QAR 449 a month." *Why:* punchy, with the price in the hero. But it is product-only and loses the company idea.
