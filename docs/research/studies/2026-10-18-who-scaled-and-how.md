## Question it answered
Which companies grew a product for small businesses through content, word of mouth, partners and loops built into the product, what did they actually do, and what carries over to ALYR? It covers 21 cases. Reddit and X could not be read, so practitioner voices come from Indie Hackers, founder podcasts, newsletters and company pages. Most numbers are self-reported.

## Key findings
- **Paid small-business customers almost never come from views.** In every case, the first paying customers came from the founder selling in person, warm introductions, or doing the setup for the owner. Podium's founders walked into Utah shops each morning in 2014 and signed about 10 businesses before building, then went from about $1M to $10M ARR in about a year (self-reported, SaaStr). lemlist's founder closed the first 100 customers himself and set up their campaigns for free; $0 to $10M ARR in about 3 years, bootstrapped (self-reported).
- **The strongest organic engine is the customer's own customers.** Calendly's shared links (about 70% of new users, unverified), Hotmail's footer (8.5M+ subscribers in about 17 months), GlossGenius's booking links in stylists' Instagram bios (70,000+ businesses; about $100M revenue in the 12 months to April 2024, self-reported) and Fresha's marketplace all turned each use into an ad.
- **The second engine is people who already visit small businesses**: accountants (Gusto pays 0% to 20% by tier), resellers and referral partners (Foodics, 40,000+ restaurants), integrators (Kommo, "5,000+ partners") and affiliates (ManyChat pays 30% to 50%).
- **A direct salon competitor is already in Qatar.** Fresha, once free, now lists an "Independent" plan at QAR 149.95 a month in Qatar.
- **WhatsApp tools for small businesses grew through app stores and integrations.** Interakt: 1,000 customers in year one, 10,000+ by June 2023 via the Shopify App Store. Wati: 6,000+ customers in 78 countries in 2022 (now claims 16,000+), helped by Shopify's investment. In the Gulf the equivalent is Salla (80,000+ merchants, 400+ apps) and Zid (12,000+).
- **Short video works when it targets the buyer, uses one repeatable format and keeps going for months.** Teta reached 140,000 registered users, 80% organic, from daily TikToks, but they were free, mostly consumer signups with no retention data. The big slideshow and faceless-account numbers (136M views in 260 days) are consumer apps reported by a vendor.
- **The most common failure is measuring the wrong audience.** A founder's build-in-public posts drew only other founders; "applause from builders" did not convert (Indie Hackers, June 2026). Another solo founder's $58K MRR came from coffees with about five people at a time, not posting (Indie Hackers, July 2023). His earlier salon app failed because salons are low-margin and slow to sell to.
- **Other risks.** Personality brands are exposed when actions contradict them: Duolingo removed all its videos in May 2025 after an AI backlash. Ad platforms over-claim credit, so track sources yourself.
- **Top ranked lessons for ALYR:** (1) in-person selling as the main engine, one district at a time, 8 to 10 visits a day, with a written "set me up at launch" note; (2) sell the setup, not the software; (3) a tasteful ALYR mark on pages the client's customers see (kept out of WhatsApp message text unless Meta allows it); (4) booking pages good enough for owners' Instagram bios; (5) every post ends in "send [word] to our WhatsApp", with a keyword per channel for attribution; (6) real results from the agent dashboard as content, once customers exist; (7) 5 to 10 referral partners (bookkeepers, PROs, freelance social managers), with a 10% to 20% share signed only after the CR.
- **Do not do:** faceless account networks, auto-voiced reposts, bought followers, competitor-attack pages, prices before the CR, or partner agreements signed before the CR.

## What we decided because of it
- Nothing directly. It fed the unmerged growth plan's strategy (in-person first, the "Booked with ALYR" mark, bio-link pages, referral partners).

## What changed since, and conflicts
- The cases and numbers still stand. The search budget left some cases out (Ryanair, Gong, Rippling, Toast and Square referral terms, Booksy, Zoko, Gallabox, Tamara).
- Its lesson 2 suggests "we set it up for you in 24 hours". That conflicts with the held-back "live in 48 hours" style of promise (D-025) and with product truth: there is no time promise on the public site. D-046's "Set up for you" is the wording in use.
- Its lesson 17 says to be open about "what ALYR's AI does". That conflicts with D-035, D-056 and D-048: there is no AI in the product and nothing says or implies AI.
- It names clinics among target buyers. D-037 says no clinics. D-043 widens the target to every owner-run business.
- Its lesson 14 (founder LinkedIn, the 100-day rule) conflicts with D-043 (on branch `origin/company-introduction`, not merged): the brand speaks alone.
