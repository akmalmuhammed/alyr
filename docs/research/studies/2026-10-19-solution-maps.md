## Question it answered
Which money-making solutions, already proven elsewhere, could ALYR offer along a shop's path from being found to being paid, and what would ALYR become with no limits?

## Key findings
- Map 1, "the floor": 15 solutions that a company in the global revenue-models study already earns from, laid on four stages (get found, take the order, get paid, keep customers) with layers underneath that earn from suppliers, lenders and new customers (the table's rows also cover supply, finance, network, service and cost). Payment links come first, because the other streams grow out of them.
- Map 1 prices to test, built on the QAR 449 plan and QAR 150 per added product: storefront and shared inbox in the plan; an AI order taker, credit ledger reminders and loyalty as QAR 150 add-ons; click-to-WhatsApp ads run for the shop at QAR 500 setup plus QAR 300 a month; a payment fee of 0.5% or a share of the partner's fee; a supplier-paid ordering channel at QAR 1,500 to 3,000 a month; a marketplace fee of 15% of a new customer's first order, capped at QAR 250; paid setup of QAR 750, refunded under the money-back promise; WhatsApp messages passed through at Meta's rate.
- Map 2, "the ceiling": stop building WhatsApp software and build a commerce network with five sides (shops, customers, suppliers, money, brands) and 16 solutions. Examples: an AI sales employee (QAR 1 per paid order it closes), an AI collections agent (1.5% of overdue money recovered), "Ask Alyr" as one number for the city, an occasion planner (5 to 8% booking fee), automatic restocking, a buying club (2 to 5% supplier rebate), invoice finance and shop advances through licensed lenders, offers in receipts, and demand insights. Rows marked "new bet" have no company earning from them yet.
- Three bets it said set Map 2 apart: AI judged on paid orders and debts recovered, seeing both the shop's sales and its purchases, and customers starting at ALYR rather than at a shop.
- Map 2 build order, with gate numbers it calls proposed targets: (1) an AI employee in every shop, until 50 paying shops with 60% of orders paid through ALYR; (2) the supplier network, until 5 paying suppliers and 300 shops ordering; (3) the customer network, until 1,000 shops in three verticals and a licensed lender signed; (4) money and media, until the network is profitable in Qatar; (5) the next country.
- Revenue stack, all assumptions: one bakery with QAR 60,000 monthly sales, 60% paid by link, brings QAR 779 a month under Map 1 and QAR 1,314 under Map 2 (about 70% more). One wholesaler selling on credit to 150 shops brings QAR 4,500 a month. At 1,000 shops and 50 wholesalers, about QAR 18.5M a year.
- Sources: it rests on "WhatsApp and small business revenue models: global study" (19 Oct) and names the companies that prove each model (Owner.com, Nuvemshop, Respond.io, Interakt, Choco, Pepper, Avoca, Boulevard, Fresha, Shopify, Toast, Square, Khatabook, Birdeye, Yalo, Moniepoint, Booksy, Zepto, Meta, SleekFlow, Slang, Stripe Connect). It gives no figures beyond those, except Meta's click-to-WhatsApp ad growth of 50 to 60% a year.

The text and tables were read in full. Of its three diagrams, the Map 2 build order was read; the Map 1 and Map 2 pictures were not opened, but the tables beside them carry the same content.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Positioning: D-048 makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several. That is closer to Map 1 than to Map 2's "commerce network", and nothing has adopted the network.
- Setup fee: the QAR 750 paid setup conflicts with D-034 (setup from QAR 1,500, quoted at the visit) and D-032 (draft at QAR 2,500). Refunding it under the money-back promise conflicts with D-034, where the refund is the first month's subscription, never the setup fee.
- Pricing: from QAR 449 a month, QAR 150 per added product (D-034, D-035), quoted per client (D-032), paid before products open (D-051). No free plan or trial; no prices or contracts before the commercial registration.
- Payments: any fee on payments, split payments and deposits must keep hard rule 3 (ALYR never holds or moves merchant funds); "deposits held by the payment partner" would need checking against it. "Alyr payment layer: analysis and strategy" (20 Oct) covers this ground.
- AI: D-035 bars saying or implying AI until a model is in the product. Customer data sent to an AI service must stay in Qatar (hard rule 2). "ALYR sector playbooks" (18 Oct) notes Meta bars general-purpose assistants from 15 January 2026.
- The marketplace fee on a new customer's first order matches the network phase in "Alyr go-to-market: priority businesses, their tech stack, and the solution map" (19 Oct). The overall solution list overlaps "Alyr Coverage Map" (19 Oct).
- Expansion: phase 5 overlaps "Alyr global expansion plan" (20 Oct) and "Selling across the GCC from Qatar" (20 Oct). Target remains every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043).
