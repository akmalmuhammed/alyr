## Question it answered
Does ALYR need a US or other foreign company to sell its software worldwide, which markets outside the GCC fit a WhatsApp-first product, and in what order should it expand without slowing Qatar? The study marks facts it could not confirm as "(unverified)" and says tax and company-law points need a lawyer and an accountant before any filing.

## Key findings
- No foreign company is needed now. The Qatar company can sell anywhere once the commercial registration (CR) is issued. A USD 500 Stripe Atlas company would only buy Stripe billing, a USD bank account and a shell US investors accept, and each can be had more cheaply or later.
- First foreign sales go through a merchant of record such as Paddle, which collects VAT and sales tax in the UK, EU and US and pays out to a Qatar bank. Fee about 5% + USD 0.50 per sale (unverified). Paddle does not exclude Qatar but refuses Iraq, Yemen, Syria, Sudan, Iran and Russia. Stripe does not serve Qatar at all, for companies or individuals.
- First market outside the GCC is the UK, not the US. WhatsApp reaches about 78% of UK mobile internet users against 28% in the US (phone-verif, rounded estimates from one aggregator). English works and the UK–Qatar treaty gives 0% on dividends. A UK Ltd costs GBP 100 (from 1 Feb 2026), with identity checks for directors since 18 Nov 2025. Open it only after 10 paying Qatar shops. It also unlocks Stripe Connect for UK shops.
- A Delaware C-corp only when a term sheet asks for it, then Atlas plus a "flip" so the Delaware company owns the Qatar W.L.L. Risks of the flip: possible loss of the GCC-owner exemption from Qatar's 10% tax, US tax on Qatar profit under the NCTI rules (the former GILTI), no US–Qatar tax treaty (30% withholding on dividends), legal cost of a few thousand to tens of thousands of dollars (unverified). Gulf investors may prefer an ADGM, DIFC or QFC holding company (QFC application fee USD 500, cut from USD 5,000 in Feb 2025). Delaware LLC, Estonia OÜ and Hong Kong were rejected; Singapore kept for later as a hub for Indonesia and Malaysia.
- Two traps for a foreign company run from Doha: it can be treated as a Qatar permanent establishment and taxed at 10% anyway, and Qatar withholds 5% on service fees paid to non-residents.
- Market ranking: 1 GCC (Kuwait, UAE, Saudi), 2 UK, 3 Spain (93% reach), 4 Netherlands and Italy, 5 Egypt, 6 Mexico, 7 South Africa, 8 Germany, then India, Brazil, Indonesia and Malaysia, Nigeria and Kenya through partners, and the US last ("raise money here, don't sell here"). France and the Philippines skipped. A hypothesis to test with 10 shops: ALYR's Qatar verticals map onto South Asian and Arab diaspora shops in UK cities.
- Meta prices per delivered message since 1 Oct 2026 differ about 40 times by country. At 3,000 service replies a month (2,000 above the free 1,000), a shop costs about USD 110 in Germany, 44 in the UK, 40 in Spain, 17 in Mexico, 2.80 in India. So price by country and pass Meta's cost through. Meta bars general-purpose AI assistants from the Business API since 15 Jan 2026, and sells its own Business Agent at USD 2 per million tokens.
- Rules that apply everywhere: the EU AI Act chatbot notice (in force 2 Aug 2026, fines up to EUR 15M or 3% of turnover), GDPR and UK GDPR (processing agreements, EU or UK hosting, an EU representative), EU VAT from the first euro through OSS, UK VAT with no threshold for a non-UK seller. Selling with no registration at all is ruled out: a fine up to QAR 200,000 or prison for trading without a CR (Al Tamimi), and Meta and payment providers need a registered company.
- Payment partners per region keep the Qatar rule: the shop is the merchant, paid to its own account, ALYR never holds funds. Partners named include Stripe Connect, Mollie, Mercado Pago, Razorpay, Paymob, Paystack and Xendit, most marked unverified.
- Staged plan with gates: Stage 0 (now to CR, about USD 100 for domains): AI notice, per-country prices, currency, language and data region, a data processing agreement. Stage 1 (CR to 10 paying Qatar shops): Meta verification, IP assigned to the Qatar W.L.L., founder vesting, Paddle. Gate A: 10 paying Qatar shops. Stage 2: GCC by remote sale, then a UK pilot of 10 shops in one vertical and one city (yearly UK cost roughly GBP 1,500–3,000, unverified). Gate B: 10 paying UK shops with Meta fees under about 15% of the subscription. Stage 3: Spain, Mexico, Egypt or Jordan. Stage 4: the holding company at the first priced round. Stage 5: high-volume markets by partner or licence.
- Founder choices it records (20 Oct): sell worldwide from the Qatar company alone, with global ads for a self-serve product starting at the CR (GCC, UK, Egypt, Jordan first) while Qatar stays sales-led; global availability as a go-live item (Linear epic ALY-181, final gate ALY-190: three test shops abroad from ad click to first paid order, alone). New issues ALY-178 (AI notice), ALY-179 (country settings), ALY-180 (Paddle, blocked by the CR); comments added to ALY-29 and ALY-32.
- Sources it cited: Rho, Zenind, InCorp, Beancount, DWF, Marks & Clerk, Eesti Firma, Leftright, Business Dubai, YB Case, QFC (company setup); PwC tax summaries, Dodo Payments, Fonoa, Numeral, Anrok, VATCalc (tax); PEX, Kennedys, Streamlex, Jackson Lewis, AZB (privacy and AI); YCloud, Kanal, Tech Times, Meta developer docs, phone-verif, Bravery (WhatsApp); Paddle, Stripe, StartupOwl, private.law (payments); Al Tamimi, Lemon Squeezy, Polar (selling without a company). It builds on the GCC study, the global revenue model study and the payment layer study.

## What we decided because of it
- Nothing in the decisions log that we know of. The study records the founder's 20 Oct choices (sell worldwide from the Qatar company, global availability at go-live, the Stage 0 issues), but no D-number carries them.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Target: every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043). Markets beyond the GCC are not a decision. The ranking here, and the "global ads from the CR" choice, sit ahead of that order and need the founder to confirm them in the log.
- Positioning: D-048 makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several and a check-up visit as the one action. A remote self-serve product sold by ads abroad does not fit that model, which depends on the visit.
- Pricing and payment: the study uses "from QAR 449" (D-034), which still holds. But D-032 quotes each client from the back office, D-051 opens products only after payment recorded by ALYR's staff (bank transfer, no online payment yet), and D-034 has the founder selling in person. A shop abroad that signs up and pays through Paddle alone is not possible under these today. No prices or contracts before the CR, which the study respects.
- AI wording: the study asks for an "AI assistant of the shop" notice in every chat. D-035 says nothing says or implies AI until a model is in the product. The EU notice applies only once AI replies exist.
- Payment partners: the study cites "Tap first, SADAD, MyFatoorah as reserve" from D-044 and the payment layer study. The hard rules in CLAUDE.md name SADAD, SkipCash and Dibsy. Read "Alyr payment layer: analysis and strategy" (20 Oct) for the current view.
- Overlaps: "Selling across the GCC from Qatar" (20 Oct) covers Stage 2's GCC part in detail; "WhatsApp and small business revenue models: global study" (19 Oct) is its input on revenue models; "ALYR Company Master Plan" (16 Oct) section 3 notes the small Qatar market and the country-pack architecture this plan relies on.
- Many facts are marked unverified: Paddle's fees, Mercury and Wise for a Qatar resident, Stripe Connect for EEA shops, the QFC annual fee, several countries' VAT rules and most partner APIs.
