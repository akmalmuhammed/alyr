## Question it answered
How should ALYR grow at launch through social media, content and other growth plays, using how platforms really spread content in 2026, while it cannot sell, show prices or run ads before the commercial registration (CR)? It builds on the founder's Brand & Growth Playbook (11 Oct) and replaces its content and social parts. It was never merged or approved, and later decisions have overtaken parts of it.

## Key findings
- Goal: customers, not followers. The one number that matters is qualified owner conversations a week: a WhatsApp chat, DM or email from someone who runs a business in Qatar. Every link and QR carries a source code (for example `alyr.qa/?s=ig-p05`), and every new owner is asked where they heard of ALYR.
- What produced paying small-business customers for others was not views. It was in-person selling, doing the setup for the owner, and the product showing up in front of the owner's own customers (research 02, 21 cases: Podium, lemlist, Calendly, Hotmail, GlossGenius). Content makes those faster. It does not replace them.
- Channels, in order of priority, with Qatar ad reach from DataReportal Digital 2026: Instagram (1.70M, +13%), founder LinkedIn (1.80M members), TikTok (widest reach), Snapchat (1.24M, +20%, young Qatari nationals), Facebook (2.40M), WhatsApp Channel and Status, YouTube Shorts (2.31M), X (650K, falling 11%), Google Business Profile, Threads. About 12 to 14 original pieces a week from one shoot and one design session.
- The bar in Qatar is low: local small-business fintechs have about 3,000 to 12,000 followers. A founder's post beats a brand's post: the SkipCash CEO's post got 146 reactions, the company's post on the same news got 17.
- Five content pillars before the CR: Owner playbook 35%, Product truth 25%, Build with us 25%, Doha rhythm 10%, Shop-floor stories 0 to 5% (rising to 30% after the CR, with written consent). Named series: Owner playbook No. N, FIG. N, Rule of the week, Shipped (Thursdays), Friday rush, On the road, Myth / fact.
- What spreads in 2026 (research 01, 04): sends per reach drive Instagram reach to new people; the first 3 seconds decide a Reel; carousels win engagement (9x the saves of single images, Metricool 2026) and Reels win reach; original work only; keywords over hashtags (Instagram caps at 5); no links in Facebook or LinkedIn post bodies; replies lift engagement about 21% (Instagram) and 30% (LinkedIn); 73% trust ads less when they suspect AI (Harris, June 2026).
- Look: the site's design system carried into posts (Paper, Carbon, Volt once per image as a signal, Instrument Sans, IBM Plex Sans Arabic, IBM Plex Mono, the 45° cut corner, FIG captions, drawn chats with no WhatsApp logo). Templates are built in code in a local studio (`D:\alyr-video\social`), with a Figma plugin kit because the Figma Starter plan's 20 calls a month were used up.
- Twelve growth plays beyond social, ranked: the founder's visits district by district, with non-binding "set me up at launch" notes collected with privacy-law (PDPPL) consent; a "Booked with ALYR" mark on booking and order pages; booking pages good enough for owners' Instagram bios; a counter QR kit; payment partners; the QDB incubation hub and QSTP; an owners' WhatsApp Community; Web Summit Qatar (31 Jan to 3 Feb 2027); referrals; click-to-WhatsApp ads (free 72-hour window); Doha micro-creators; pages per business type; a data story after 6 to 9 months.
- Phases: 0 foundations (claim handles, bank of 20 to 30 approved posts, five questions to the lawyer), 1 soft launch before the CR (no price, offer or "sign up"), 2 launch after the CR, 3 scale (Saudi Arabia next, Snapchat first at 73% reach). Early targets, to be tested: 10 qualified conversations a month by the end of Phase 1, saves plus sends above 3% of reach on carousels, 1,000 engaged followers in Qatar before launch.
- Five questions for the lawyer: marketing before the CR, the Ministry of Culture advertising licence (about QAR 5,000 a year), Arabic in ads under Law No. 7 of 2019, PDPPL consent for leads, and creator disclosure.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- **Founder-led content conflicts with D-043** (19 Oct, accepted by the founder, on branch `origin/company-introduction`, not merged): the brand speaks alone, with no founder name or photo on the site, deck or profiles, and people on visits are "the ALYR team". The plan's founder LinkedIn and X as channel 2, the "On the road" series, founder-to-camera videos and its recommendation that the founder's face and voice lead (section 14, item 6) all go against this.
- **Positioning moved on.** The plan uses "We do the tech. You run the business." and the suite with "Payroll coming". D-047 (22 Oct) led the homepage with the owner's problem ("Stop losing orders in the chat."). D-048 (23 Oct, updated 24 Oct) now says ALYR is the small business's tech team ("The tech team your business never had."), with one action, "Book a check-up". D-046 (21 Oct) added ALYR Shelf and ALYR Docs and took Payroll off the site.
- **Words and marks.** D-048 changes "rule" to "reason/why" on the homepage, so the "RULE ·" lines in the message table and hooks need the new wording where they copy the homepage. The founder replaced FIG captions with a single "Sample" mark (brand.md, 24 Oct), so the "FIG. N" series and FIG caption design need renaming.
- **Look still holds.** The Carbon and Volt look matches D-040, which retired the playbook's cobalt look. The section 13 table agrees.
- **Prices still off.** D-034 keeps prices off public channels until the CR, and D-051 (23 Oct) says clients pay before products open. The plan's "no price, no offer, no sign up" before the CR still matches. Its "price set per client" line is now "from QAR 449 a month, setup from QAR 1,500" (D-034), still not public.
- **Target.** The plan lists six business types and no clinics (matches D-037). D-043 widens this to every owner-run business, with the sector recorded on each lead.
