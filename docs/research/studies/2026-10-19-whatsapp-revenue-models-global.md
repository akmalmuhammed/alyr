## Question it answered
How do companies around the world make money from small businesses through WhatsApp and similar software, which models work and which failed, and what should ALYR change because of it?

## Key findings
- Small businesses pay most for software that sits in their flow of money. Shopify earns 78% of its revenue from payments and finance (payments on 68% of sales), Toast 82%. Lending is the second engine: Square funded USD 7B of loans in 2025, Shopify USD 1.58B in Q2 2026 alone.
- It ranks ten proven models: 1 payments on every order, 2 advances repaid from sales, 3 the brand or distributor pays for the ordering channel, 4 a simple subscription of USD 50 to 300 a month plus add-ons, 5 an AI agent priced on outcomes in one vertical, 6 a fee on new customers a network brings, 7 commission on every order, 8 ads, 9 a small fixed device fee, 10 paid setup.
- Meta makes the real money on WhatsApp: paid messaging passed a USD 2B yearly run rate in Q4 2025. Resellers run thin. Jio Haptik (Interakt) spends 63% of revenue buying messages; Gupshup's valuation fell from USD 1.4B to about USD 300M at a 3.9% EBITDA margin; Yellow.ai cut about 30% of staff.
- The best small business vendor, Respond.io, reached USD 35M ARR at a 30% margin with plans of USD 79, 159 and 279 a month and no markup on messages. SleekFlow charges USD 999 for onboarding.
- Generic chatbots are becoming free. Meta Business Agent went global on 3 June 2026 and is free in the India Business app; on the API it costs about 4 to 5 US cents a message. Vertical agents that complete a paid outcome charge USD 250 to 600 per location a month (Avoca reached a USD 1B valuation; Slang.ai USD 450 to 600).
- Supplier-pays works: Yalo has 1M+ Coca-Cola FEMSA shops ordering free on WhatsApp (600,000+ a month, over 15% of FEMSA's orders); Pepper handles about USD 30B of distributor orders; Choco reads orders from email, voicemail and text.
- What failed: reselling messages, free ledgers with no payments (Khatabook's loss larger than its revenue; BukuWarung 8M users, 250,000 active), holding stock (MaxAB-Wasoko, Sabi), expensive seat pricing (Podium from about USD 3B to about USD 0.5B), marketplaces before the tool, generic AI that only answers, and high take rates (TikTok Shop about 47% in year one).
- Meta pricing changes: per-message billing from 1 July 2025; from 1 October 2026 in-window utility messages are charged again and service replies are charged after 1,000 free per number per month. Usernames and business-scoped user IDs are rolling out in 2026.
- Keep for ALYR: the QAR 449 starting price (about USD 123, inside the USD 50 to 300 band and below the USD 400+ level where Podium struggled), a capped one-time new-customer fee rather than commission, never holding funds or stock, tool before marketplace.
- Change for ALYR: track the share of orders paid through an ALYR link and put a payment link in every order, invoice and reminder; ask Tap, MyFatoorah and SkipCash for a share of their fee rather than adding 0.5% on top; make the supplier pay in the wholesale lane; sell the AI as an order taker that turns voice notes and photos of lists into priced orders; pass message costs through at cost with an allowance per plan; charge for setup instead of free setup for the first 25 shops per vertical.
- Order of work it proposed: subscription, payment link on every order and a paid setup now; then supplier-pays wholesale and AI order intake; click-to-WhatsApp ad packages after 10 paying shops; the Occasions marketplace for Ramadan 2027; lending referrals and promoted listings once there is payment data. Technical risk: customer records should accept the business-scoped ID as well as a phone number before the end of 2026.
- Sources it cited: Meta WhatsApp pricing documentation (read 19 Oct 2026), Meta 10-Q Q2 2026 and Q4 2025 earnings call, SEC filings and results for Shopify, Toast, Block, ServiceTitan, MercadoLibre and Weave, Jio Haptik FY25 statements, Respond.io Series B (Business Wire), Paytm and GoTo results, TechCrunch, Entrackr, Inc42, Latka and Sacra (estimates), 360dialog, and earlier research notes. Not found: revenue for Yalo, WATI, Nuvemshop, Charles and Superchat, and Meta's prices for paid business tiers.

## What we decided because of it
- Nothing directly. Its "keep" items match decisions already taken (D-034: from QAR 449, a setup fee, no free plan or trial). Its "change" items are open.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Price: from QAR 449 a month, setup from QAR 1,500 (D-034), quoted per client (D-032), paid before products open (D-051). D-017's 490 and 890 plans are superseded. No prices or contracts before the commercial registration.
- Setup: the study's call to charge for setup is settled by D-034. The "free setup for the first 25 shops" it argues against conflicts with D-034, which has no free plan or trial.
- Payment revenue share: still open. Hard rule 3 (non-custodial) holds either way. See "Alyr payment layer: analysis and strategy" (20 Oct), which covers the same providers in depth.
- Positioning: the study is WhatsApp-centred. D-048 now makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several, so read its models as inputs to the whole suite (D-035).
- The capped new-customer fee and the Occasions marketplace come from earlier plans ("Alyr: Qatar Go-To-Market and Scale Plan", 11 Oct; "Alyr growth and content playbook", 20 Oct). Target is every owner-run business, Qatar first then the GCC (D-043); the wholesale and Tier 2 references are from those plans.

## Use it for
- Any decision on revenue lines beyond the subscription: payment revenue share, supplier-pays wholesale, lending referrals, ads, paid ad setup.
- Arguing for a low base price and against message markup or seat pricing.
- Meta's 2026 pricing and username changes, as context. Check Meta's current pricing page before relying on the figures.
