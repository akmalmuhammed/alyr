## Question it answered
How should ALYR enter Qatar and grow for its first 18 months: which businesses first, what to sell, at what price, through which channels, and with what targets? It assumed a bootstrapped or small-seed start (under USD 500k), 2 to 4 founders and early hires, Arabic and English from day one, and Qatar first with GCC expansion from month 12. Every claim is tagged [S] (sourced) or [A] (assumption to test in pilots).

## Key findings
- Recommendation: launch as "your own commission-free ordering and payment channel inside WhatsApp" for independent restaurants, cafes, bakeries and cloud kitchens in Doha (1 to 5 branches), sold door to door with a free pilot. Add booking (salons, clinics), marketing and capital later on the same core.
- Market [S]: population 3.21m, about 88 to 90% expats (NPC, Dec 2025); internet 99.0% (DataReportal 2026); WhatsApp used by 87% of residents and 97% of Qataris, but that figure dates from 2014; about 71,000 SMEs, 97% of private companies (Gulf Times); commercial registrations up 81.5% year on year in Q3 2025; USD 4.4bn of digital payments in one month (QCB data, Jul 2025). [A] 15,000 to 25,000 SMEs in target sectors; Qatar alone supports about USD 3 to 8m ARR for this category, so the GCC is needed for scale.
- Segment scores (out of 25, [A]): restaurants and cafes 22 (wedge, months 0 to 6); social sellers 20 (self-serve from month 3); salons 20 (months 6 to 9); home services 19; clinics 18 (month 9+, health-data rules); fitness and education 16; retail with a website 16; trading and B2B distributors 15 (year 2).
- Why food first: delivery apps charge 15 to 30% commission (vendor blogs, to confirm in interviews) in a Qatar delivery market of about USD 1bn (Redseer). [A] Moving 15% of a QAR 60,000-a-month delivery business to WhatsApp saves about QAR 1,800 to 2,700 a month. Ordable sells web ordering in Qatar at QAR 3,000 to 4,500 a year, not inside WhatsApp.
- Comparables [S]: Toast's fintech gross profit is 1.6x its subscription gross profit, 80% field sales, 20% of deals from referrals; Shopify Payments handle 66% of GMV; Square 81% transaction revenue; Foodics went cafe tablets, POS, Pay, then Capital; Kommo grew WhatsApp connections from 1,000 to 25,000 on Coexistence; respond.io USD 35m ARR with resellers on 15 to 40% commission. Warning cases: Gupshup (enterprise messaging) and Dukaan (free micro-merchant sign-ups did not become revenue).
- Competitors in Qatar [S]: Ordable (high threat), Mawidi (WhatsApp booking receptionist, high for booking), Elokra (QAR 199 to 499 a month), DineNORDER, Wasl POS (QAR 299 to 799, better as a partner), SkipCash and SADAD (substitutes or partners), Wati and similar remote inboxes, Fresha in salons. Zbooni (3.5% + AED 1 per order) is not in Qatar yet and is the model to learn from. No funded player sells ordering, booking, payment and order management all inside WhatsApp to Qatar SMBs.
- Pricing [A]: Start QAR 199, Grow QAR 449 (core plan), Pro QAR 899 a month; setup free in pilots, then QAR 500; two months free on annual plans; a 0.5% payments share negotiated with the gateway; Meta fees passed through at cost. Targets: about QAR 600 revenue per customer a month, CAC about QAR 2,000, payback about 4 months, churn 3% a month (6% risk case, ChartMogul median 6.1% at low prices), LTV/CAC about 8x (4x at 6% churn), gross margin about 85%.
- Sales engine [A]: founder-led, then field sellers doing 8 to 12 visits a day; live demo on the owner's own number; "live in 48 hours" pilot of 14 days with card on file. Funnel: contact 50%, demo 27%, pilot 46%, paid 64%. Month-12 channel mix: field 45%, referrals 20%, partners 20% (20 to 30% recurring commission), inbound 15%. Pilot success: 30 WhatsApp orders or QAR 3,000 direct revenue in 14 days.
- Targets [A]: day 90, 15 paying restaurants and at least 50% pilot conversion; month 12, 300 customers and about USD 550k ARR; month 18, 700 customers, USD 1.4m ARR and monthly breakeven (at about 480 to 600 customers). Peak burn about QAR 1.16m (USD 320k). Kill signal at day 90: under 30% pilot conversion or under 15 WhatsApp orders a month per merchant.
- Qatar specifics [S]: Meta per-message pricing since July 2025; from 1 Oct 2026 only 1,000 free service messages a month per number and a 7-day free window after click-to-WhatsApp ads; start as a Meta Tech Provider, Solution Partner at about 100 customers. General-purpose AI assistants banned on WhatsApp since 15 Jan 2026. No native WhatsApp payments in Qatar, so use links from a QCB-licensed gateway and never hold funds. PDPPL (Law 13 of 2016): 72-hour breach reporting, prior permission for health data, fines up to QAR 5m.
- Programmes: apply to MCIT SMEs Go Digital in month 1 (59 approved providers), QDB Scale7 in month 2, the Startup Qatar Investment Program after 10+ paying pilots; showcase at Web Summit Qatar (31 Jan to 3 Feb 2027).
- Top risks: small-merchant churn above 5% a month, Meta pricing or policy changes, a merchant's number banned for spam, delivery-app retaliation, a competitor moving first, Qatar too small, founder bandwidth.
- Sources it cited: DataReportal, NPC via Marhaba, Gulf Times, The Peninsula, IBS Intelligence (QCB data), Indian Embassy brief, Redseer, QNA, QDB, NDS3, QIA, Meta docs and rate card (via Gupshup), YCloud, respond.io, Infobip, Al Tamimi, Qatar Tribune, MoCI guidelines, Toast S-1 and Q2 2026, SaaStr, Foodics, Shopify, Block, HubSpot 10-K, Wati, Interakt, ChartMogul, Lenny's Newsletter, Sixteen Ventures, Hunter. It listed as not verifiable: Qatar delivery commission rates, a current WhatsApp usage share, SMB counts by sector, gateway fees, and Meta's exact Qatar rate after 1 Oct 2026.

## What we decided because of it
- No decision names this plan as its source. Its QAR 449 core plan matches the later starting price (D-032, D-034), and its Tech Provider route, non-custodial payments through SkipCash, Dibsy or SADAD, and Qatar hosting match the hard rules, but the decisions do not cite it.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Target: not restaurants first. D-024 (same day, 11 Oct) named businesses that book, order, pay and track; the target is now every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043). The master plan's section 3 had named credit traders and service businesses first.
- Positioning: D-048 makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several, not "a commission-free WhatsApp channel". D-035 made ALYR a suite of products.
- Pricing: the 199 / 449 / 899 tiers, the QAR 500 setup and the annual discount are replaced by "from QAR 449 a month, setup from QAR 1,500" (D-034), quoted per client (D-032), with QAR 150 for each added product (D-035). D-017's 490 / 890 plans are also superseded.
- Pilots: the free 14-day pilot, free design partners and "live in 48 hours" conflict with D-034 (no free plan or trial; "live in 48 hours" stays held back) and D-051 (the client pays before products open). The money-back first month replaced the pilot.
- Not decided: the 0.5% payments share, field-seller hiring, the funding path and the Capital product.
- No prices or contracts before the commercial registration (D-034); this plan does not account for that.
- Read with: "ALYR sector playbooks", "Alyr go-to-market: priority businesses, their tech stack, and the solution map" (segments); "WhatsApp and small business revenue models: global study" (comparables); "Alyr payment layer: analysis and strategy" (gateway revenue share); "Alyr growth and content playbook" (channels); "Selling across the GCC from Qatar" and "Alyr global expansion plan" (GCC); "Client onboarding plan" (pay before access replaces the pilot).
