## Question it answered
Which sales, SaaS, behavioural-science and game-design tactics actually work, which are ethical and legal in Qatar and safe under WhatsApp policy, and what content plan follows for ALYR before and after the commercial registration (CR)? It also proposed a reusable content skill, described separately.

## Key findings
- Main conclusion: the tactics that hold up are the boring ones. Remove friction, show real proof, make progress visible, ask for a small first step. Clever persuasion tricks are often smaller or less durable than their reputation. Each tactic is graded Strong, Moderate or Weak.
- Endowed progress (moderate to strong): in a car wash field study, 34% redeemed a card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-filled against 19% for an empty 8-stamp card (Nunes and Dreze 2006). Use: the setup checklist starts at "2 of 8 done".
- Defaults (strong for choices, weak for outcomes): in a 32,500-student field test, an opt-out default moved registration only 1.5 points (91.5% vs 90.0%) and did not change who sat the exam (Cambridge, 2022). Use: pre-fill sensible settings, never pre-tick marketing consent.
- Social proof (moderate): the hotel towel study, about 44% vs 35% reuse (Goldstein, Cialdini, Griskevicius 2008). Use real shops and real counts only. Choice overload is weak (Scheibehenne et al. 2010 meta-analysis found an average effect near zero). Loss aversion is moderate and contested. Several rows were written "from memory" and the study says to confirm them before quoting publicly.
- SaaS practice: time to first value (the first order received on WhatsApp), founder-led onboarding for the first 10 to 50 customers, a demo before the sales call, two-sided referral paid in service credit, and a weekly WhatsApp summary as the retention engine, since most churn is decided in the first 30 days. Sales talks diagnose first (missed messages, late replies, double bookings), quantify the cost, then offer a small paid first step.
- Game mechanics: borrow progress bars, levels and real milestones fully; streaks only weekly, with a pause and no penalty; real surprises tied to real work only. Never near-misses, loss chasing, dark patterns or paid randomness. Avoid shop-vs-shop leaderboards. Evidence linking loot boxes to problem gambling is serious but not settled (PeerJ 2019, 112 players).
- Hard limits: gambling is illegal in Qatar, any customer prize draw needs a ministry permit (to confirm with the lawyer on ALY-32), WhatsApp bans real-money gambling messaging in Qatar and requires opt-in for marketing. A four-question ethics test: is the reward real, can the person stop with one tap, would they be comfortable seeing how it works, does it work with no money at stake.
- Content plan: one line, "Every message answered, every order captured, on the WhatsApp your customers already use." Five pillars: the lost-order moment 25%, behind the counter 25%, the product at work 20%, owner wins 15%, know-how 15%. Eight hooks in English and Gulf Arabic (Arabic marked as drafts for a native reviewer). Post ideas per channel (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, WhatsApp Status to opted-in contacts, YouTube Shorts, a 5-email waitlist drip).
- Before the CR, every call to action is "join the waitlist, we onboard you personally"; after it, "start your paid first month, money back if we miss the result". Offers are service, not free product: a free 5-minute WhatsApp audit, a done-for-you menu upload, the money-back guarantee.
- Homepage outline (pain hero, proof strip, problem band, live demo, four steps, vertical tabs, pricing, FAQ, waitlist form with WhatsApp number and shop name only) and vertical pages for bakeries (first), wholesale traders, catering, homegrown businesses and licensed rentals. Bakery offer: money back if the shop does not receive 20 orders in 30 days, the number agreed per shop.
- Activation nudges with one metric each, from a reply within 1 minute of joining the waitlist to one-tap cancel with a one-line reason. A 30-day waitlist calendar ending in Ramadan prep (Ramadan about 8 Feb 2027). First metrics: signups per channel, menu received within 24 hours, setup completed, first order within 7 days, week-4 active shops. Change one variable at a time and wait for a few hundred visitors.
- Sources it cited: Nunes and Dreze 2006 (via Silicon Canals), Kivetz et al. 2006, a Cambridge Behavioural Public Policy paper on defaults (2022), Goldstein, Cialdini and Griskevicius 2008, Cialdini's Influence, Kahneman and Tversky 1979, Gal and Rucker 2018, Scheibehenne et al. 2010, Ariely, Duolingo (company claims), Paul Graham's "Do things that don't scale", Dropbox's referral case, PeerJ 2019, the WhatsApp Business policy.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Positioning: D-048 makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several and "Book a check-up" as the one action. The playbook's single WhatsApp-ordering message, waitlist button and homepage outline are replaced by that. Its "free 5-minute audit" is close to the check-up; whether the check-up is free is still open for the founder (D-048).
- Target: every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043). The playbook's "bakeries first, then catering, homegrown, rentals" order and the master plan's credit traders (section 3) are both narrower; D-024 had already widened the first buyer to businesses that book, order, pay and track.
- Pricing: "3 plans from QAR 449" and "annual saves months" do not match D-032 (one quote per client from the back office, billing periods of 1, 3, 6 or 12 months) or D-034 (month to month). "Founding-shop pricing locked for 12 months" is not decided. The old 490/890 plans of D-017 are superseded. No prices or contracts before the CR.
- Money back: D-034 counts one agreed result over the first 30 days after going live, and refunds the first month's subscription, never the setup fee. D-051 adds that the client pays before products open. The bakery's "20 orders in 30 days" fits only as the agreed result for that shop.
- Cancelling: "one-tap cancel" conflicts with D-034's term: stop at the end of any month with 7 days' notice.
- AI words: the playbook allows "agentic" wording (ALY-154) and a pillar of AI reading orders. D-035 and D-048 say nothing says or implies AI until a model is in the product.
- Overlaps: "Alyr self-serve onboarding audit" (20 Oct) and "Client onboarding plan" (23 Oct) cover the onboarding nudges; "ALYR sector playbooks" (18 Oct) and "Alyr go-to-market: priority businesses, their tech stack, and the solution map" (19 Oct) cover the verticals; "Alyr: Qatar Go-To-Market and Scale Plan" (11 Oct) covers channels.
- Still sound: the evidence grading, the ethics test, the Qatar and WhatsApp limits, opt-in only broadcasts, native Arabic writing and the measurement rules.
