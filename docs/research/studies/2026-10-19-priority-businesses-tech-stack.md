## Question it answered
Which kinds of business should ALYR approach first, what tools do they already use, and how should ALYR cover each layer of a small business over time?

## Key findings
- Approach three types now, because they fit what is built and can pay QAR 449 a month: bakeries and dessert shops, wholesale traders who sell on credit, and abaya, perfume and gift boutiques. If only one: bakeries and dessert shops (easiest demo, National Day on 18 Dec and Ramadan as deadlines, future Occasions supply), with wholesalers as a second lane.
- Tier 2, talk now and pilot after one build: caterers and event setups (need quotes, deposits and capacity per date; the bet for Ramadan 2027, around 8 Feb), home services (need technician calendars and quotes), and cafeterias and baqalas (need a lower price). Tier 3, not now: salons (bookings serve one customer at a time; Fresha in Qatar since July 2025), restaurants living on delivery apps, clinics (health data not checked by the lawyer), travel and typing offices (inbox only).
- First 20 conversations: 8 bakeries, 6 wholesalers, 6 boutiques, plus 5 caterer interviews counted separately. Pilot with one type, not three. Starting district suggested as Al Sadd and Bin Mahmoud, marked as an assumption.
- State on 19 Oct: inbox, Catalog, orders, price groups, credit accounts and the Agent dashboard worked on Meta's test number. Three blockers to the first paid pilot: a payment provider (ALY-17), the shop's own WhatsApp number (ALY-39, backup ALY-77) and always-on hosting (ALY-10, ALY-63).
- Typical stack: the WhatsApp Business app on one phone, Instagram, a bank card terminal, payment links or Fawran transfers, and Excel or Tally. Bigger shops add Foodics (about QAR 250 to 300 per terminal a month), Zoho or a delivery app. Fawran handled QAR 6.745bn in 3.886m transfers in June 2026, up 145% (Gulf Times). QCB caps debit fees at 1.1%, or 0.5% for micro merchants (SADAD). WPS fines reach QAR 6,000 per breach (Doha Guides).
- Pitch rule: never ask a shop to drop a tool on day one. Every answer is "we work beside it, connect to it, or replace it later". It gives a ten-row objection table, including "QAR 449 is expensive" answered with 20% commission on QAR 2,500 of repeat orders being QAR 500 a month.
- Check before promising: Meta's coexistence (one number on the app and the platform at once) must be confirmed for Qatar numbers on ALYR's provider route.
- Solution map of 22 layers: 6 covered, 4 partly, 11 gaps, and the card terminal left to partners for good. ALYR owns where customer and money meet (talk, catalog, orders, credit, reports); each new layer should reuse an order, customer or payment ALYR already holds. Money, terminals, accounting and salary payments stay with licensed partners.
- Roadmap in five phases with gates: go live (to Nov 2026, first pilots), ten paying (Nov 2026 to Jan 2027; delivery fees, statements, broadcasts, quotes and deposits), Occasions (Feb to Apr 2027), back office (mid 2027; Zoho, QuickBooks and Tally export, WPS file, POS link), network (late 2027; marketplace fee on a new customer's first order only). Until 10 paying customers, build only what a paying pilot needs (the founder's rule of 16 Oct).
- Risks it named: payments are the bottleneck, not features; QAR 449 shuts out cafeterias, baqalas and home kitchens, so plan a smaller paid plan after 10 customers while keeping no free tier.
- Sources it cited: the ALYR repo (docs/status.md, docs/decisions.md, operating model, presets), the project playbook files, SADAD, Gulf Times, Menuviel, Fresha, Doha Guides, EY, Wafeq and Qatar Living.

The text was read in full. Of its three diagrams, the roadmap was read; the priority map and the solution-map picture were not opened, but the tables beside them carry the same content.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- Target: every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043). The tiering is a sales order of approach at most, not a limit on who ALYR serves.
- Positioning: D-048 makes ALYR the small business's tech team, with WhatsApp one product among several. The study's "chat-to-cash core" framing fits inside that.
- Pricing: still from QAR 449 a month (D-034, D-035 combination pricing), setup from QAR 1,500 quoted per client (D-032, D-034). The client now pays before products open (D-051). No free plan or trial, and no prices or contracts before the commercial registration; its pilot steps must wait for that.
- The 22-layer map overlaps "Alyr Coverage Map" (19 Oct) and "Alyr solution maps" (19 Oct). Payment partners overlap "Alyr payment layer: analysis and strategy" (20 Oct).
- "ALYR sector playbooks" (18 Oct) puts beauty and field services in wave 2; this study parks salons. Both hold clinics back.
- Its state table is a 19 Oct snapshot; check docs/status.md before quoting what is built.
