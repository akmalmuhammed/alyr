## Question it answered
What would a fundable business plan look like for ALYR's first product in Qatar? The draft guessed that the product was construction software for mid-size contractors, and it marked most lines "Assumption" for the founder to confirm. BuildStack was an early working name. It is not the company's name.

## Key findings
- Goal it set: become the operating system for Qatar's mid-size contractors, with 100 paying companies and about QAR 4.2M of annual recurring revenue by the end of year 3, funded by a QAR 7M (about USD 1.9M) seed round lasting about 30 months.
- Market: Qatar construction worth QAR 198.4bn in 2026 (QAR 190.5bn in 2025), forecast QAR 243bn by 2031, 4.1% a year (The Peninsula, citing Mordor Intelligence). 78% of spending is public, 63% is in Doha, commercial work is 35% of revenue, renovation grows 6.1% a year, about 86% of activity uses traditional on-site methods. Ashghal's pipeline is about 900 projects worth about QAR 250bn and runs on Oracle Primavera Unifier.
- Addressable market, marked "estimate, to verify": about 1,500 contractors paying about QAR 40,000 a year, so about QAR 60M a year in Qatar.
- Target buyer: a Doha contractor with 50 to 500 staff running 3 to 20 projects. Then specialist subcontractors (20 to 200 staff), then fit-out firms (10 to 100). Daily users are expatriate site engineers and surveyors, so mobile-first, bilingual and simple matter more than features.
- Competition: the real rival is Excel plus WhatsApp. Procore (entered MENA in 2021 with Mubadala) and Oracle Aconex/Unifier are built and priced for large firms. Generic ERPs (Odoo, SAP Business One) do not cover site work. Regional startups were left "to research".
- Placeholder pricing: Starter QAR 1,500 a month, Growth QAR 4,500, Enterprise from QAR 15,000; onboarding QAR 5,000 to 25,000; 15% off for annual prepayment. Planning average QAR 3,500 per customer per month.
- Go-to-market: founder-led sales in Doha, five design partners free or at half price for months 0 to 6, then an Arabic-speaking sales lead, events (CONTEQ, Project Qatar), the Qatar Chamber and contractors' association, and partners (accountants, PROs, ERP resellers, banks). Proof point: days from claim to payment.
- Legal: recommended a 100% foreign-owned QFC entity; the QFC application fee fell from USD 5,000 to USD 500 in February 2025 (Qatar Tribune). Mainland LLC setup about 2 to 8 weeks and QAR 5,000 to 15,000 in fees (Qatar Living). QSTP terms "to verify".
- Team and money: 6 people in year 1, 10 in year 2, 16 in year 3. Customers 15, 45, 100; ARR QAR 540k, 1,782k, 4,200k; net loss about QAR 2.0M to 2.4M each year. Investors named: Qatar Development Bank programmes, QSTP, VCs backed by the QIA USD 1bn fund of funds.
- Top risk: site staff not adopting a new tool over WhatsApp. Others: long sales cycles with family firms, a small home market, price cuts by Procore or Oracle, slower government spending, data residency on public projects.
- Sources it cited: The Peninsula, Turner and Townsend Qatar Market Intelligence 2025, Oracle (Ashghal), Business Wire (Procore), Qatar Tribune, Qatar Living, Gulf Business.

## What we decided because of it
- Nothing directly. The draft rested on a guess about the product that turned out wrong. Its open questions (what the product does, who pays, stage, team, raise, registration) were answered by the later plans.

## What changed since, and conflicts
- The product is WhatsApp-native tools for owner-run businesses, not construction software. The 11 Oct go-to-market plan and the 16 Oct company master plan replaced this plan.
- Target: every owner-run business, Qatar first, then the GCC (D-043). The master plan (section 3) first named credit traders and service businesses; D-024 widened the first buyer to businesses that book, order, pay and track.
- Pricing: from QAR 449 a month, setup from QAR 1,500 (D-034), quoted per client (D-032), paid before access (D-051). There is no free or half-price design-partner phase (D-034 has no free plan or trial). No prices or contracts before the commercial registration.
- Funding: the later plans are product-first and paid from the first customer (D-034), not a QAR 7M seed raise.
- Still useful in parts: the legal setup options (QFC, mainland LLC, QSTP) and the risk that owners and staff stay on WhatsApp habits.

## Use it for

- The early comparison of QFC, mainland LLC and QSTP setup routes. Check the fees again before relying on them.
