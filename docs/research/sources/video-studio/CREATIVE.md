# ALYR product films: creative direction

Three films, made 18 Oct 2026: **ALYR Bookings**, **ALYR Chat**, **ALYR Agent**. Each is a 60-second master at 16:9, built so it also cuts to 9:16 and 1:1. The words are chosen for one person: an owner in Doha who runs a shop from their phone.

## 1. Who we're talking to

The owner of a salon, a bakery, an AC company, a shop. They are serving a customer while the phone buzzes. They already run the business on WhatsApp, because their customers do. They are not looking for software. They are looking for:

- **customers who don't slip away:** the order lost in the chat, the booking at 11 pm that nobody saw;
- **fewer empty hours:** the customer who booked and didn't come;
- **their time back:** confirmation calls, copying orders into a notebook, chasing who owes what;
- **knowing where they stand,** without a spreadsheet;
- **looking professional** to their customers.

They distrust anything that sounds like tech. They trust what they can see working on a phone, a price of time they can feel, and a person they can call.

## 2. What sells (and what we refuse)

From the research (sources at the end):

| What works | How we use it |
|---|---|
| Hook in the first 3 seconds with the viewer's own life, not the product | Every film opens on a moment the owner has lived, in their words |
| Assume sound is off | The story is told entirely in type. Music and sound carry feeling, never meaning |
| Show the real product early; outcomes beat feature lists | The real screens, with the sample businesses, from second 10. Every feature is stated as what it changes for the owner |
| One action solves one problem, not a feature tour | Each beat is one moment: a booking made, an order confirmed, a rule shown |
| Short | 60 s masters; 15 s and 6 s cuts come from the same scenes |

**What we refuse, because it would cost us trust:**
- No invented numbers about other businesses: no "cut no-shows by 89%". We show the owner's own numbers, from the sample business, labelled as samples.
- Nothing that isn't built. These stay out:
  - deposits;
  - card payments (built on a test page, not live);
  - several chairs at once;
  - messages to quiet regulars;
  - anything still in a "Not yet" list.
- No AI words. ALYR Agent counts and compares by rules; the films say exactly that.
- No WhatsApp logo or green. The chat is drawn neutral; the word WhatsApp is used.

## 3. The campaign line

**We do the tech. You run the business.** It closes all three films. Each film has its own opening thought and its own product line.

| Film | Opening thought | Product line (the site's own words) |
|---|---|---|
| Bookings | **Booked. Didn't come.** | Customers book in the chat. ALYR reminds them before they come. |
| Chat | **The order came in the chat. Then it got lost in the chat.** | Orders in the chat. One inbox for the team. |
| Agent | **You know your business. Do you know your week?** | Your business in numbers. What needs you, first. |

## 4. The look: modern, and unmistakably ALYR

The 2026 vocabulary, inside ALYR's rules:

1. **Type you read like a headline.** Huge, tight, sentence case. Lines rise from a mask. The second line is grey, so the eye reads the first.
2. **Two sides of one moment.** The customer's phone and the owner's workspace, side by side, reacting to the same tap. This is the proof that it's one system.
3. **The clock.** A mono clock jumps forward (14:21 → 17:30). It's how we show what happens without anyone touching anything.
4. **Macro on the moment.** A fast push into the one thing that matters (the reminder, the rule), then stillness. No drifting cameras.
5. **The fold.** Three Carbon planes along 45° turn every page. It's ALYR's own device, used where other brands use a flash.
6. **Volt is a signal, never decoration:** the tap, the confirmation, the current state. If everything is Volt, nothing is.
7. **Motion rules:**
   - everything arrives along the logo's 45° diagonal on a long, smooth settle (expo.out);
   - cuts happen at full speed with motion blur;
   - nothing bounces, nothing breathes, nothing floats.

**Colour by film,** within the brand: all three use Paper and Carbon, and the light/dark rhythm differs.
- **Bookings** is warm daylight (Paper), with Carbon for the moments of tension.
- **Chat** has more Carbon: the busy counter, the team at work.
- **Agent** opens on Carbon and ends on Paper: from the noise to the clear view.

**Sound.** Composed in code, one track per film, and every sound effect lands on a frame:
- Bookings: calm, 100 BPM, major, with a clock tick.
- Chat: brisk, 118 BPM, driving, with a soft pop for every message.
- Agent: precise and minimal, 108 BPM, a counting pulse.

All three are mixed to −14 LUFS.

## 8. The rules each film is checked against before it ships

- Every product shot carries a FIG caption naming the sample as fictional.
- Every line stays on screen for at least its word count ÷ 3 seconds, plus 1 second.
- The contrast check passes, and no text is clipped or overlapping (HyperFrames `check`).
- Every claim matches `copy.ts` and the "Not yet" lists on the day it's published.
- Nothing is posted before the CR. These are files for the founder, not posts.
