# Customer agreement: notes for the founder and the lawyer

Written 17 Oct 2026 by the Company lane. Linear: ALY-98 (under ALY-32). Decision: D-034 (proposed). The agreement is [agreement.md](agreement.md).

**This is a draft for a Qatar-qualified lawyer to check (ALY-32, ALY-51). It is not legal advice.** Where these notes describe Qatari law or Meta's rules, they say where the reading comes from and how sure it is. No lawyer has seen any of it.

## In one minute

- The agreement states the founder's commercial terms of 16 and 17 Oct as he set them: from QAR 449 a month, setup from QAR 1,500, month to month, the first month's money back against one agreed result, no commission, Meta's fees billed by Meta. It prints on four A4 pages at 10 pt, schedule and signatures included.
- Everything the founder did not set is a proposal, listed in section 1. **The three to check first:**
  1. **The setup fee is refunded in full if ALYR cannot get the shop live** within 30 days of the agreed date (7.4(c)). ALYR cannot yet connect a shop's own number, so this protects both the owner and ALYR's name.
  2. **7 days' notice to stop** (7.2), except in the first month. The page says only "stop at the end of any month": either the page adds "with a week's notice" or the agreement drops the lead time.
  3. **Arabic and English side by side, Arabic prevailing** (13.5). Qatari courts work in Arabic, so the Arabic is what a judge reads anyway; it must be a checked legal translation before anyone signs.
- **Registration: no, ALYR cannot lawfully sign this or take money before it is registered** (section 2). Trading without a CR carries up to 6 months' prison, a fine of up to QAR 200,000, or both. A contract signed now would bind the founder personally, not the company, and banks and payment providers ask for a CR. With "paid from the first customer" and no free pilot, **the CR is now the gate to the first sale**: file ALY-59 now, and until then sign nothing and take nothing.
- Before any owner signs: the `TO FILL` items, the Arabic version, the lawyer's review, and the product fixes in section 4. The back office still promises "your first month is free", which contradicts clause 5.

## 2. Can ALYR sign this and take payment before it is registered?

**Short answer: no.** As far as the research found, ALYR cannot lawfully sign customers or take their money until it holds a commercial registration, and it needs the e-commerce licence to sell through its website. Anything signed before then binds the founder personally, not the company. A lawyer must confirm this (question 1 in section 5); it is research, not advice.

| Question | What the research found | Source (section 7) | How sure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Can anyone trade before registering? | No. "No natural or legal person may engage in commerce" before being entered in the commercial register (art. 7). Up to 6 months' prison and/or a fine of up to QAR 200,000, and the court may close the business (art. 16); the case can be settled by paying half the maximum fine (art. 16 bis) | Commercial Register Law No. 25 of 2005, as amended by Law No. 20 of 2014 | Official text read |
| Does selling by subscription count? | Advertising a business to the public makes someone a trader (art. 13); only a one-off transaction does not (art. 14). A priced monthly subscription offered to many shops is not one-off | Commercial Law No. 27 of 2006 | Official text read; applying it to ALYR is inference |
| Can the company sign before it exists? | A company has no legal personality until it is publicised (art. 8). An LLC "may not commence any of its business" until it is entered in the register (art. 233). People who contract in the name of a company that has no legal form are personally and jointly liable (art. 5). No article was found that lets an LLC take over a contract signed before registration | Commercial Companies Law No. 11 of 2015, as amended by Law No. 8 of 2021 | Official text read; "no adoption" means none found |
| Selling online | No commercial activity through a website without MOCI's e-commerce licence (art. 2). The applicant must already be registered (art. 3(1)). The licence holder must show its CR and licence numbers, contact details, a description of the service, an exchange and return policy, and how complaints are handled (art. 5). In force since 16 Mar 2026; "website" includes social media; the fee is reported as QAR 500 | Ministerial Decision No. 25 of 2026; Al Tamimi and Crowell notes | Official text read; the fee is from news reports |
| Foreign ownership | Economic activity in breach of the foreign-investment law carries a fine of up to QAR 500,000 (art. 20). The approved company must be registered before it carries out the project (Executive Regulations, art. 3) | Law No. 1 of 2019; Decision No. 44 of 2020 | Official text read |
| Taking payment | QNB's and SADAD's merchant services require a CR. The government's e-commerce portal says every business, online ones included, must apply for a CR. Payment into a personal account is still trading without a CR, and keeps the founder personally liable | Providers' own pages; ecommerce.gov.qa | Providers' pages read; Dibsy and SkipCash publish no list |
| The founder's own residence | A work residence permit is tied to the employment contract with the recruiter (art. 4); working for others without permission is an offence (arts. 23, 38) and can cost the permit (art. 37(3)). The law does not say whether a permit holder may own or manage their own LLC. These notes assume nothing about the founder's status | Law No. 21 of 2015, as amended by Decree-Law No. 19 of 2020 | Official text read; how it applies is for the lawyer |

**What to do.**

1. **Make registration the gate to the first sale.** The 16 Oct decision is "paid from the first customer" with no free pilot, so no owner can start until ALYR has a CR. File ALY-59 now. The ministry's route for foreign capital: apply through the Invest in Qatar Center on the Single Window; the activity is checked against the permitted list; then passports, a beneficial-owner declaration and an incorporation document notarised by the Ministry of Justice; then the CR, the commercial licence and the e-commerce licence. The law gives 15 days for the decision on foreign ownership above 49% (Law No. 1 of 2019, art. 3) and 15 days to register an LLC (Companies Law, art. 233); in practice, reports range from a few days to several weeks.
2. **Until the CR:** show owners the agreement and the price, and agree the result and a go-live date in principle. Sign nothing as ALYR, and take no setup fee, deposit or subscription, into any account. If a shop wants to commit, the most to sign is a short non-binding letter of interest, worded by the lawyer.
3. **Ask the lawyer about the homepage.** The new page shows prices and an offer. Whether publishing it before the CR counts as holding out as a trader (Commercial Law, art. 13) is a fair question. If it might, publish the priced page the day the CR is issued. The live site (12 Oct) shows no price.
4. **Who signs for ALYR.** The founder's project plan for the Single Window (a PDF on his laptop, not in git) proposes a single-person LLC and names its owner. Whoever is the owner of record, the person who signs for ALYR must be its appointed manager or hold a written authority (question 2 in section 5).
5. **After the CR:** fill the `TO FILL` items. Put the trade name and CR number, in Arabic, on the agreement, invoices and letters (Law No. 25 of 2005, art. 6; fine up to QAR 50,000 under art. 15). Issue Arabic or bilingual invoices (consumer law regulations, art. 4; the internal tool already plans bilingual invoices). Open the company's bank account, apply to payment providers (ALY-5), get the e-commerce licence, and publish the return and complaints page it requires: clauses 5 and 7.4 give that page its content.

## 5. For the lawyer

One engagement can cover this, the data questions in ALY-51 and the price sheet in ALY-32. Questions, with what we found so far:

1. **Signing before registration** (section 2): can the founder sign for a company in formation, and with what personal liability? Or must he wait?
2. **Who signs for ALYR.** If the company's owner of record is not the founder, what document lets him sign (manager appointment in the articles, or a power of attorney)?
3. **Standard-form terms.** The agreement is an adhesion contract under Civil Code art. 105, so art. 106 lets a court change arbitrary terms and art. 107 reads doubt for the customer. Which clauses are at risk: the liability cap (12), pausing (8), ALYR's right to stop (7.3), price changes (6.6)?
4. **Liability.** Does clause 12 work under arts. 259 and 263? Is "gross error" the right English for «الخطأ الجسيم»?
5. **Price changes** under arts. 150, 171 and 287: is 30 days' notice with a free exit enough?
6. **Late payment.** We chose no interest and no fee. Art. 262(1) lets parties agree that a debtor is in default on the due date without notice; should the agreement say so, given art. 81 of the Commercial Law (registered mail) otherwise?
7. **Consumer law.** Probably it applies. Consumer Protection Law No. 8 of 2008 defines a consumer as anyone who obtains goods or services for their own or others' needs, and does not exclude businesses (art. 1; a Gulf Times report says it covers companies). If so: misleading descriptions are banned, including of contract terms, price and warranty (art. 7; regulations art. 14); advertisements and the required information must be in Arabic (art. 17), and so must invoices (regulations art. 4); a defective service must be refunded or redone (art. 11); and a clause exempting the seller from these duties is void (art. 24). Fines reach QAR 1,000,000 (art. 18; Al Meezan marks that version as ended, which looks like a database error). The draft adds a line to clause 12 keeping these rights. Please confirm, and say whether the money-back headline on the site is safe. The e-commerce law's 3-day right to cancel (Decree-Law No. 16 of 2010, art. 57) seems not to apply, because that law's consumer excludes buying for business (art. 1).
8. **PDPPL.** Is clause 9.3 a sufficient controller-processor contract under the regulator's guideline (subject, duration, data, instructions, sub-processors, help with rights, audits, return or deletion)? Is Meta the owner's processor under Meta's own terms, as those terms say, and does ALYR need to say more about it? What must the owner's privacy notice to their customers say (art. 9)?
9. **Special-nature data.** Are a clinic's or salon's appointment bookings "health" or "children's" data under art. 16, needing the regulator's permit? If yes, should ALYR take clinics before that is settled?
10. **Cross-border.** Art. 15 forbids the controller from restricting cross-border flows except where processing breaks the law or may cause serious damage. Is listing the services in Part B enough? (ALY-51's main question.)
11. **Language.** Does Arabic Language Law No. 7 of 2019, art. 2(2), reach a private company's contracts? Which version should prevail? Which certified translator?
12. **Electronic acceptance.** Is a logged click in the workspace enough under Decree-Law No. 16 of 2010 (arts. 2, 4, 20, 27), and what record should ALYR keep?
13. **Courts.** Is "the competent courts of the State of Qatar" right, given the Investment and Trade Court's exclusive jurisdiction over commercial contracts (Law No. 21 of 2021, art. 7)?
14. **Records.** How long must ALYR keep its own invoices and correspondence, so 9.2's "what the law requires us to keep" can be specific?
15. **Licence activities.** Do the planned licence activities cover the subscription, the setup work and the "when you need more" jobs (ALY-32)?
16. **Meta's terms.** Does ALYR, as a provider, have to pass any of Meta's WhatsApp Business Platform terms down to the owner in this agreement?

## 6. The Arabic version

- **Why it matters.** If the consumer law applies, the information about the service and its price must be in Arabic (art. 17), and so must invoices (regulations, art. 4). The trade name and CR number must appear in Arabic on business papers (Law No. 25 of 2005, art. 6). So the Arabic version is needed before anyone signs, not later. Arabic is also the language of Qatar's courts (Civil and Commercial Procedures Law No. 13 of 1990, art. 68), and the Investment and Trade Court requires an Arabic translation of every foreign-language document (Law No. 21 of 2021, arts. 17 and 19). Whatever the clause says, a judge reads Arabic. The Arabic Language Law binds ministries and public bodies to Arabic contracts (art. 2(1)); whether art. 2(2) reaches private companies is unclear, and nothing in it makes an English-only private contract invalid. A practitioner note found by the research says private contracts are often signed in English only.
- **Recommendation.** Sign both, side by side, with the Arabic prevailing, because an owner reads Arabic and a court will. It means ALYR must own the Arabic text: a legal translator, then the lawyer, then the native reviewer (ALY-66) for plain language. The founder cannot check it himself. No machine translation.
- **The other choice.** English prevails, with an agreed Arabic translation signed alongside. ALYR controls the meaning in the language it drafts in, but the owner signs terms in a language they may read less well, and art. 107 reads doubt against ALYR anyway.
- **Words to keep consistent** with the site's Arabic and with the law's own terms:
  - setup fee «رسوم التجهيز» and go-live «بدء التشغيل», as on the page;
  - the first month's subscription «اشتراك الشهر الأول»;
  - controller and processor as the PDPPL's Arabic text names them (to confirm on Al Meezan);
  - fraud «الغش» and gross error «الخطأ الجسيم», as in Civil Code art. 259;
  - ALYR's Arabic legal name, exactly as on the CR.
- **Numbers and dates.** Western digits for amounts and dates, as the product writes them ("QAR 1,850"); Gregorian dates.
- **Length.** Side by side on the page doubles the length to about eight pages. To keep it readable at the counter, print the Arabic and the English as two booklets signed together, or the Arabic first.
- **Meta's policy names.** Use Meta's own Arabic names where Meta publishes them, otherwise the English names in brackets.
